PRACTICAL GUIDE: Bathroom Electrical Installation
================================================================================

STEP-BY-STEP BATHROOM CIRCUIT INSTALLATION:

PHASE 1: PLANNING & DESIGN

1. Count Required Circuits
   Minimum per 2022 CEC:
   □ 1× 20A receptacle circuit (GFCI required)
   □ 1× 15-20A lighting circuit (optional separate)
   
   Recommended:
   □ 1× 20A dedicated receptacle per bathroom
   □ 1× 15A lighting/fan circuit
   □ 1× 20A for heat lamp/heater (if applicable)

2. Plan Outlet Locations
   Code requirements:
   - Minimum: 1 receptacle within 3 feet of sink basin
   - Recommended: 2 receptacles (each side of sink)
   - All receptacles: GFCI required (no exceptions)
   - Height: 15-18" above counter typical
   
   Practical recommendations:
   - Dual outlet near sink (both sides)
   - Consider outlet for electric toothbrush/razor
   - Think about night lights
   - Plan for future needs (bidet, heated toilet seat)

3. Plan Lighting & Exhaust Fan
   Lighting requirements:
   - At least 1 light fixture per bathroom
   - Switch at entry point
   - Consider task lighting at mirror
   - Vanity lights popular
   
   Exhaust fan requirements:
   - Required if no window (in many jurisdictions)
   - Switch or timer control
   - May tie into lighting circuit
   - Consider humidity sensor switch

4. Check Clearances Around Water
   Critical safety requirements:
   - No receptacles in tub/shower zone
   - No switches within reach of tub/shower
   - Light fixtures: Must be rated for damp/wet location
   - Minimum clearances maintained

5. Determine Wire Sizing
   20A receptacle circuits: 12 AWG copper wire
   15A lighting circuits: 14 AWG copper wire
   
   Distance considerations:
   - Voltage drop <3% required
   - Bathroom usually close to panel
   - 14 or 12 AWG adequate for most bathrooms

PHASE 2: PERMIT APPLICATION

1. Determine if Permit Needed
   Permit REQUIRED for:
   ✓ New bathroom circuits
   ✓ Relocating outlets or fixtures
   ✓ Adding exhaust fan with new circuit
   ✓ Major bathroom remodel
   
   Permit NOT REQUIRED for:
   ✗ Replacing outlets same location (Section 82-3(a))
   ✗ Replacing light fixtures same location
   ✗ Replacing exhaust fan same location

2. Apply via EPIC-LA
   URL: epicla.lacounty.gov
   Permit type: "Electrical Permit - Bathroom Remodel"
   
   Application details:
   □ Number of new circuits
   □ Outlet locations
   □ Lighting/exhaust fan circuits
   □ Work description
   □ Contractor license # (C-10 required)

3. Pay Permit Fee
   Fee structure:
   - Base: $36.00
   - Per circuit: ~$25-50 each (estimate)
   - Typical bathroom (2-3 circuits): $100-200
   - Verify current: (626) 458-3180

4. Wait for Approval
   Timeline:
   - Simple bathroom: 5-7 days
   - Complex remodel: 10-15 days

PHASE 3: INSTALLATION (Licensed Electrician)

Receptacle Circuit (1× 20A, GFCI required):

□ Run 12/2 Romex from panel to bathroom
□ Install GFCI receptacle OR GFCI breaker at panel
□ Typical: 1-2 outlets near sink
□ All outlets must be GFCI protected
□ Height: 15-18" above counter or 12-18" above floor
□ No outlets within tub/shower zone
□ Label circuit "Bathroom Receptacles" in panel

GFCI Installation Methods:

Option 1: GFCI Receptacle
  → Install GFCI as first outlet
  → Wire additional outlets to LOAD terminals
  → All downstream outlets protected
  → Test button on GFCI outlet
  → Cost: $15-30 per GFCI outlet

Option 2: GFCI Breaker
  → Install GFCI breaker at panel
  → Entire circuit protected
  → All outlets downstream protected
  → Test button on breaker
  → Cost: $40-80 per GFCI breaker
  → Recommended for bathrooms

Lighting/Exhaust Fan Circuit (1× 15-20A):

□ Run 14/2 or 12/2 Romex from panel
□ Install switch at entry (not within reach of tub/shower)
□ Wire to light fixtures
□ Wire to exhaust fan (if present)
□ May use 3-way switches for multiple entries
□ GFCI not required (not receptacles)
□ Label circuit "Bathroom Lighting" in panel

Heat Lamp/Heater Circuit (1× 20A, if applicable):

□ Run 12/2 Romex from panel
□ Dedicated circuit recommended for heaters
□ Install timer or switch (not within reach of tub/shower)
□ Wire to heater unit
□ Verify heater clearances
□ Label circuit "Bathroom Heater" in panel

SAFETY CLEARANCES (CRITICAL):

Receptacles:
- NO receptacles within tub/shower zone
- Minimum 3 feet from tub/shower edge (outside zone)
- GFCI required for ALL bathroom receptacles

Switches:
- NO switches within reach of tub/shower
- Minimum 5 feet from tub/shower edge
- Or outside bathroom entirely

Light Fixtures:
- Fixtures within 8 feet of tub/shower: Must be rated for damp location
- Fixtures directly over tub/shower: Must be rated for wet location
- Minimum 8 feet above tub/shower (if not wet-rated)

Exhaust Fans:
- May be installed above tub/shower
- Must be rated for damp/wet location
- Proper venting to exterior required

PHASE 4: INSPECTION

Rough-In Inspection (Before Drywall):

Inspector verifies:
□ Romex properly secured and protected
□ Box locations correct (height, clearances)
□ Wire gauge appropriate (12 AWG for 20A, 14 AWG for 15A)
□ No damaged wire insulation
□ Proper box fill (not overcrowded)
□ Grounding continuity
□ No outlets in tub/shower zone
□ No switches within reach of tub/shower

If rough-in PASSES:
✓ Approved for drywall/finishes

If rough-in FAILS:
✗ Inspector notes violations
✗ Fix before covering
✗ Re-inspection required

Final Inspection (After Completion):

Inspector verifies:
□ ALL bathroom receptacles GFCI protected
□ GFCI outlets functioning (will test)
□ At least 1 receptacle within 3 feet of sink
□ Receptacles proper height
□ No receptacles in tub/shower zone
□ No switches within reach of tub/shower
□ Light fixtures properly rated (damp/wet location)
□ Exhaust fan functioning (if present)
□ All connections tight
□ Cover plates installed
□ Panel labeling complete
□ Proper outlet types (15A or 20A)

Testing by inspector:
- GFCI test button (will press on each bathroom outlet)
- Outlet tester for proper wiring
- Verify 120V at receptacles
- Light fixtures function
- Exhaust fan operates (if present)

If final inspection PASSES:
✓ Work approved
✓ Certificate issued
✓ Can use bathroom

If final inspection FAILS:
✗ Violations noted
✗ Fix and re-inspect
✗ Common: GFCI not working, outlets in shower zone

INSPECTOR CHECKLIST: Bathroom Circuits
================================================================================

WHAT INSPECTOR WILL VERIFY:

GFCI PROTECTION:
□ ALL bathroom receptacles (125V-250V) GFCI protected
□ GFCI test button works (inspector presses it)
□ GFCI trips when tested
□ GFCI resets properly
□ No exceptions (ALL bathroom outlets need GFCI)

OUTLET LOCATION:
□ At least 1 receptacle within 3 feet of sink basin
□ Receptacles proper height (15-18" above counter typical)
□ NO receptacles in tub/shower zone
□ NO receptacles within arm's reach of tub/shower
□ Outlets accessible (not blocked)

CIRCUIT REQUIREMENTS:
□ At least 1× 20A receptacle circuit serving bathroom(s)
□ 20A circuit uses 12 AWG wire
□ Dedicated circuit OR properly shared with other bathrooms
□ Lighting circuit separate OR combined (per code)
□ Proper circuit separation if dedicated

SWITCH LOCATION:
□ Light switch at entry point
□ NO switches within reach of tub/shower (5 feet minimum)
□ Exhaust fan switch accessible (may be timer)
□ Proper switch height (48" typical)

LIGHTING FIXTURES:
□ At least 1 light fixture present
□ Fixtures within 8 feet of tub/shower: Rated for damp location
□ Fixtures over tub/shower: Rated for wet location
□ Fixtures functioning properly
□ Proper mounting

EXHAUST FAN:
□ Exhaust fan present (if required by code)
□ Fan rated for damp/wet location
□ Fan functioning properly
□ Proper venting to exterior (not attic)
□ Switch or timer control

WIRING:
□ 12 AWG wire for 20A circuits
□ 14 AWG wire for 15A circuits (lighting)
□ Romex properly secured
□ No damaged insulation
□ Proper box fill (not overcrowded)
□ All connections tight
□ Grounding continuity

PANEL:
□ Bathroom circuits labeled clearly
□ Proper breaker sizes (20A for receptacles)
□ No double-tapping
□ Panel schedule accurate

CLEARANCES:
□ No electrical within tub/shower zone
□ Proper distances maintained
□ No code violations

COMMON INSPECTOR FINDINGS (Violations):

❌ VIOLATION 1: No GFCI on Bathroom Receptacles
   Problem: Bathroom outlets without GFCI protection
   Why it fails: Code requires GFCI on ALL bathroom receptacles (2022 CEC 210.8(A)(1))
   Fix: Install GFCI outlets or GFCI breaker
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(A)(1)
   Cost to fix: $15-30 per outlet or $40-80 for GFCI breaker

❌ VIOLATION 2: Receptacle in Tub/Shower Zone
   Problem: Outlet within reach of tub/shower
   Why it fails: Electrocution hazard near water
   Fix: Relocate outlet outside zone (3+ feet from tub/shower)
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.52(D) + 406.9(C)
   Cost to fix: $150-300 (relocate outlet)

❌ VIOLATION 3: Switch Within Reach of Tub/Shower
   Problem: Light switch within arm's reach of tub/shower
   Why it fails: Electrocution hazard when wet
   Fix: Relocate switch 5+ feet from tub/shower
   Reference: 2022 CEC 404.4(C)
   Cost to fix: $100-250 (relocate switch)

❌ VIOLATION 4: GFCI Not Functioning
   Problem: GFCI test button doesn't trip circuit
   Why it fails: Defective GFCI or wiring issue
   Fix: Replace GFCI or correct wiring
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(D)
   Cost to fix: $15-50 (replace GFCI)

❌ VIOLATION 5: No Receptacle Within 3 Feet of Sink
   Problem: No outlet near sink basin
   Why it fails: Code requires outlet for grooming appliances
   Fix: Install outlet within 3 feet of sink
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.52(D)
   Cost to fix: $100-200 (add outlet)

❌ VIOLATION 6: Light Fixture Not Rated for Location
   Problem: Regular fixture over tub/shower (not wet-rated)
   Why it fails: Moisture damage and electrocution risk
   Fix: Replace with wet-rated fixture
   Reference: 2022 CEC 410.10
   Cost to fix: $50-200 (fixture + installation)

❌ VIOLATION 7: 15A Breaker on Bathroom Receptacle Circuit
   Problem: Undersized breaker for bathroom circuit
   Why it fails: Code requires 20A minimum for receptacles
   Fix: Replace with 20A breaker AND verify 12 AWG wire
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.11(C)(3)
   Cost to fix: $20-50 (breaker) or $200-400 (rewire if needed)

❌ VIOLATION 8: Receptacle Polarity Reversed
   Problem: Hot and neutral wires swapped
   Why it fails: Safety hazard
   Fix: Correct wiring at outlet
   Reference: 2022 CEC 200.11
   Cost to fix: $25-50 (rewire outlet)

❌ VIOLATION 9: Exhaust Fan Not Vented to Exterior
   Problem: Fan vented to attic instead of outside
   Why it fails: Moisture damage, mold growth
   Fix: Reroute vent ducting to exterior
   Reference: Building code (not electrical)
   Cost to fix: $150-400 (duct work)

❌ VIOLATION 10: Missing Circuit Labeling
   Problem: Panel doesn't show bathroom circuits
   Why it fails: Code requires circuit directory
   Fix: Create accurate labels
   Reference: 2022 CEC 408.4
   Cost to fix: $25-75 (circuit identification)

Q: "Do I need GFCI in my bathroom?"
A: "Yes. ALL bathroom receptacles (125V-250V) require GFCI protection (2022 CEC 210.8(A)(1)). 
This includes every outlet in bathroom space, no exceptions. Options: (1) GFCI outlets 
($15-30 each) or (2) GFCI breaker at panel ($40-80). Inspector WILL test GFCI 
functionality. Replacing outlets same location: no permit. New circuits: permit required."

Q: "How many circuits do I need for bathroom?"
A: "Minimum: 1× 20A receptacle circuit (GFCI required). Recommended: 1× 20A dedicated 
receptacle + 1× 15A lighting/fan circuit = 2 circuits. Master bathroom: Add 1× 20A for 
heat lamp/heater = 3 circuits. All bathroom receptacles must be GFCI protected. Permit 
required for new circuits via EPIC-LA: epicla.lacounty.gov"

Q: "How much does bathroom electrical remodel cost?"
A: "Typical costs: GFCI upgrade only ($95-220), Single bathroom with 2 circuits 
($915-1,935), Master bathroom with 3-4 circuits ($1,760-4,080). Most common: $1,400 for 
standard bathroom. Includes permit ($100-200), materials ($190-535), labor ($625-1,250). 
Get 3 quotes from licensed C-10 contractors."

Q: "Can I put outlet in shower?"
A: "NO. Receptacles cannot be installed in tub/shower zone. Minimum 3 feet from tub/shower 
edge. This is life-safety requirement (electrocution risk). If outlet currently in shower: 
Must be relocated outside zone. Inspector will fail this. Reference: 2022 CEC 406.9(C). 
Cost to relocate: $150-300."

Q: "What does inspector check for bathroom outlets?"
A: "Inspector verifies: (1) ALL bathroom receptacles GFCI protected, (2) GFCI test button 
works (will press it), (3) At least 1 outlet within 3 feet of sink, (4) NO outlets in 
tub/shower zone, (5) NO switches within reach of tub/shower (5 feet minimum), (6) Light 
fixtures wet-rated if over tub/shower, (7) At least 1× 20A receptacle circuit. See 
cross-reference-bathroom-wiring-LA.txt for complete checklist."

Q: "My bathroom GFCI failed inspection. Why?"
A: "Top 3 failures: (1) GFCI test button doesn't trip (defective GFCI - replace $15-50), 
(2) Outlet in tub/shower zone (must relocate 3+ feet away $150-300), (3) Switch within 
reach of tub/shower (relocate 5+ feet away $100-250). Get inspector's report, fix 
violations, request re-inspection through EPIC-LA."

Q: "Can I replace bathroom outlets without permit?"
A: "Yes, if replacing outlets in same locations (Section 82-3(a)). This is minor repair 
exempt from permit. However, ALL bathroom outlets MUST be GFCI protected (no exceptions). 
Strongly recommend upgrading to GFCI if not already present. If adding new circuits or 
relocating outlets: Permit required."

Q: "Do I need special light fixtures in bathroom?"
A: "Yes. Fixtures within 8 feet of tub/shower: Must be rated for damp location. Fixtures 
directly over tub/shower: Must be rated for wet location. Regular fixtures will fail 
inspection if in these zones. Check fixture rating label. Wet-rated fixtures: $50-200. 
Reference: 2022 CEC 410.10."
