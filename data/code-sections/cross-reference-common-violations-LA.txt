CATEGORY 1: GFCI PROTECTION VIOLATIONS (Most Common Overall)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 1: No GFCI on Kitchen Counter Receptacles
❌ Problem: Kitchen countertop outlets without GFCI protection
   Why it fails: All counter outlets require GFCI (2022 CEC 210.8(A)(6))
   Where: Kitchen remodels, new construction
   How to fix: Install GFCI outlets or GFCI breaker at panel
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(A)(6)
   Cost to fix: $15-30 per GFCI outlet OR $40-80 for GFCI breaker
   Prevention: Plan GFCI protection for ALL kitchen counter outlets

VIOLATION 2: No GFCI on Bathroom Receptacles
❌ Problem: Bathroom outlets without GFCI protection
   Why it fails: ALL bathroom receptacles require GFCI (2022 CEC 210.8(A)(1))
   Where: Bathroom remodels, new bathrooms
   How to fix: Install GFCI outlets or GFCI breaker
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(A)(1)
   Cost to fix: $15-30 per outlet OR $40-80 for breaker
   Prevention: GFCI mandatory for EVERY bathroom outlet (no exceptions)

VIOLATION 3: No GFCI on Garage Receptacles (LA County Specific!)
❌ Problem: Garage outlets without GFCI, especially freezer outlets
   Why it fails: LA County requires GFCI on ALL garage receptacles
   LA County specific: No exception for freezers/dedicated circuits
   Where: Garages (most common LA County garage failure)
   How to fix: Install GFCI outlets or GFCI breaker
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(A)(2) + LA County amendment
   Cost to fix: $15-30 per outlet OR $40-80 for breaker
   Prevention: Remember LA County is MORE restrictive than state code

VIOLATION 4: No GFCI on Outdoor Receptacles
❌ Problem: Outdoor outlets without GFCI protection
   Why it fails: ALL outdoor receptacles require GFCI (2022 CEC 210.8(A)(3))
   Where: Exterior outlets, patios, decks
   How to fix: Install GFCI outlets or GFCI breaker
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(A)(3)
   Cost to fix: $20-40 per WR GFCI outlet OR $40-80 for breaker
   Prevention: GFCI + weatherproof covers required outdoors

VIOLATION 5: GFCI Test Button Not Working
❌ Problem: GFCI outlet doesn't trip when test button pressed
   Why it fails: Defective GFCI or incorrect wiring
   Where: Any GFCI location (inspector tests EVERY GFCI)
   How to fix: Replace GFCI or correct wiring (line/load reversed)
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.8(D)
   Cost to fix: $15-50
   Prevention: Test GFCI before requesting inspection

CATEGORY 2: AFCI PROTECTION VIOLATIONS (Very Common in New/Remodel Work)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 6: Missing AFCI Breakers on Bedroom Circuits
❌ Problem: Bedroom circuits without AFCI protection
   Why it fails: Code requires AFCI for bedrooms and living areas
   Where: New construction, bedroom additions, panel upgrades
   How to fix: Install AFCI breakers on bedroom circuits
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.12
   Cost to fix: $40-70 per AFCI breaker
   Prevention: Check code year - AFCI required in newer homes

VIOLATION 7: Standard Breakers Used Instead of AFCI
❌ Problem: Regular breakers on circuits requiring AFCI
   Why it fails: Living areas, dining rooms need AFCI protection
   Where: Family rooms, dining rooms, hallways, closets
   How to fix: Replace with AFCI breakers
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.12
   Cost to fix: $40-70 per breaker
   Prevention: Understand AFCI requirements for living spaces

CATEGORY 3: GROUNDING & BONDING VIOLATIONS (Safety Critical)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 8: Only One Ground Rod (Need Two Minimum)
❌ Problem: Service entrance has only one ground rod
   Why it fails: Code requires 2 ground rods minimum (6 feet apart)
   Where: Service upgrades, new construction
   How to fix: Install second ground rod, bond together
   Reference: 2022 CEC 250.53(A)(2)
   Cost to fix: $100-200
   Prevention: Always install 2 ground rods for services

VIOLATION 9: Neutral-Ground Bond at Sub-Panel
❌ Problem: Neutral and ground bonded together in sub-panel
   Why it fails: Creates parallel neutral path (safety hazard)
   Where: Sub-panels, detached structures
   How to fix: Remove neutral-ground bond from sub-panel
   Reference: 2022 CEC 250.142
   Cost to fix: $50-150
   Prevention: Bond neutral-ground ONLY at main service panel

VIOLATION 10: Inadequate Solar Array Grounding
❌ Problem: Solar array frame not properly grounded to earth
   Why it fails: Lightning protection and safety requirement
   Where: Solar PV installations
   How to fix: Install proper grounding electrode system
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.43-690.47
   Cost to fix: $200-500
   Prevention: Follow solar grounding requirements carefully

VIOLATION 11: Missing Grounding Electrode Conductor
❌ Problem: No ground wire from panel to ground rods
   Why it fails: No path to earth ground (safety hazard)
   Where: Service panels, sub-panels
   How to fix: Install grounding electrode conductor to ground rods
   Reference: 2022 CEC 250.64
   Cost to fix: $200-500
   Prevention: Verify ground wire properly sized and connected

CATEGORY 4: WIRE SIZING & BREAKER VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 12: 20A Breaker on 14 AWG Wire
❌ Problem: Breaker too large for wire gauge (fire hazard)
   Why it fails: 14 AWG wire rated for 15A maximum
   Where: Kitchen circuits, garage circuits, any 20A circuit
   How to fix: Replace with 15A breaker OR rewire with 12 AWG
   Reference: 2022 CEC 240.4(D)
   Cost to fix: $20 (breaker) OR $200-500 (rewire)
   Prevention: 20A circuit = 12 AWG minimum, 15A circuit = 14 AWG OK

VIOLATION 13: Undersized Service Entrance Conductor
❌ Problem: Wire too small for service amperage rating
   Why it fails: Fire hazard from overheating
   Where: Service upgrades (100A→200A)
   How to fix: Replace with properly sized conductor
   Reference: 2022 CEC Table 310.12
   Cost to fix: $300-800
   Prevention: 200A service requires 2/0 copper or 4/0 aluminum minimum

VIOLATION 14: Wrong Wire Gauge for Circuit Length
❌ Problem: Voltage drop exceeds 3% on long runs
   Why it fails: Too small wire for distance
   Where: Detached structures, long outdoor runs
   How to fix: Upsize wire to reduce voltage drop
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.19
   Cost to fix: $200-1,000 depending on length
   Prevention: Calculate voltage drop for runs >100 feet

CATEGORY 5: PANEL & BREAKER VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 15: Double-Tapped Breakers
❌ Problem: Two wires connected to single breaker terminal
   Why it fails: Loose connection risk (unless breaker rated for 2)
   Where: Panels with insufficient spaces
   How to fix: Install separate breaker OR use rated breaker
   Reference: 2022 CEC 110.14
   Cost to fix: $50-150
   Prevention: One wire per breaker terminal

VIOLATION 16: Missing Circuit Directory
❌ Problem: Panel doesn't show what each breaker controls
   Why it fails: Code requires circuit identification
   Where: All panels (very common violation)
   How to fix: Create accurate circuit directory
   Reference: 2022 CEC 408.4
   Cost to fix: $50-100
   Prevention: Label circuits during installation

VIOLATION 17: Inadequate Panel Working Clearances
❌ Problem: Storage or obstructions in front of panel
   Why it fails: Must have 36" × 30" × 6.5' clearance
   Where: Garages, basements, utility rooms
   How to fix: Remove obstructions, maintain clearances
   Reference: 2022 CEC 110.26
   Cost to fix: $0-500 (move items)
   Prevention: Plan panel location with clearances in mind

VIOLATION 18: Panel Located in Bathroom or Closet
❌ Problem: Panel installed in prohibited location
   Why it fails: Not permitted by code
   Where: Older homes, poor planning
   How to fix: Relocate panel to acceptable location
   Reference: 2022 CEC 240.24(D)
   Cost to fix: $1,500-4,000 (major relocation)
   Prevention: Never install panels in bathrooms or clothes closets

VIOLATION 19: Incompatible Breakers in Panel
❌ Problem: Wrong brand breakers (e.g., Square D in GE panel)
   Why it fails: Safety issue, may not function properly
   Where: DIY installations, incorrect replacements
   How to fix: Replace with manufacturer-approved breakers
   Reference: 2022 CEC 110.3(B)
   Cost to fix: $50-200
   Prevention: Match breaker brand to panel brand

CATEGORY 6: OUTLET LOCATION & SPACING VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 20: Kitchen Counter Outlet Spacing Exceeded
❌ Problem: More than 4 feet between counter outlets
   Why it fails: Code requires outlets every 4 feet maximum
   Where: Kitchens
   How to fix: Add outlets to meet spacing requirement
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.52(C)
   Cost to fix: $75-150 per additional outlet
   Prevention: Plan outlet spacing during design

VIOLATION 21: No Receptacle Within 3 Feet of Bathroom Sink
❌ Problem: No outlet near sink basin for grooming appliances
   Why it fails: Code requires outlet for personal care
   Where: Bathrooms
   How to fix: Install outlet within 3 feet of sink
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.52(D)
   Cost to fix: $100-200
   Prevention: Plan sink area outlet during bathroom design

VIOLATION 22: Missing Front or Back Outdoor Outlet
❌ Problem: House doesn't have required outdoor receptacles
   Why it fails: Code requires minimum 1 front + 1 back
   Where: Older homes, new construction oversights
   How to fix: Install missing outdoor outlet(s)
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.52(E)
   Cost to fix: $150-400 per outlet
   Prevention: Verify front + back outdoor outlets in plans

VIOLATION 23: Receptacle in Tub/Shower Zone
❌ Problem: Outlet within reach of tub or shower
   Why it fails: Electrocution hazard near water
   Where: Bathrooms (dangerous mistake)
   How to fix: Relocate outlet 3+ feet from tub/shower
   Reference: 2022 CEC 406.9(C)
   Cost to fix: $150-300
   Prevention: Maintain clearances from water sources

VIOLATION 24: No Garage Receptacle Present
❌ Problem: Garage has no electrical outlet at all
   Why it fails: Code requires minimum 1 receptacle in garage
   Where: Garages, carports
   How to fix: Install GFCI-protected garage outlet
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.52(G)
   Cost to fix: $100-200
   Prevention: Include garage outlet in plans

CATEGORY 7: WEATHERPROOFING & OUTDOOR VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 25: Non-Weatherproof Covers on Outdoor Outlets
❌ Problem: Standard indoor covers used outdoors
   Why it fails: Must be weatherproof in-use type
   Where: Exterior outlets (extremely common)
   How to fix: Replace with weatherproof in-use covers
   Reference: 2022 CEC 406.9(B)
   Cost to fix: $10-30 per cover
   Prevention: Use in-use covers (stay closed with plug inserted)

VIOLATION 26: Non-Weather-Resistant Receptacles Outdoors
❌ Problem: Standard receptacles instead of WR type
   Why it fails: Outdoor requires weather-resistant (WR) receptacles
   Where: All exterior locations
   How to fix: Replace with WR-rated receptacles
   Reference: 2022 CEC 406.12
   Cost to fix: $15-30 per receptacle
   Prevention: Look for "WR" marking on outdoor receptacles

VIOLATION 27: Outdoor Boxes Not Sealed Against Weather
❌ Problem: Outdoor boxes not caulked or sealed
   Why it fails: Water intrusion risk
   Where: Exterior outlets, light fixtures
   How to fix: Caulk all box penetrations
   Reference: 2022 CEC 314.15
   Cost to fix: $25-75
   Prevention: Caulk during installation

VIOLATION 28: Indoor Light Fixtures Used Outdoors
❌ Problem: Non-exterior rated fixtures installed outside
   Why it fails: Must be wet or damp location rated
   Where: Exterior walls, soffits, patios
   How to fix: Replace with exterior-rated fixtures
   Reference: 2022 CEC 410.10
   Cost to fix: $50-200 per fixture
   Prevention: Check fixture rating (wet/damp location required)

CATEGORY 8: UNDERGROUND WIRING VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 29: Improper Underground Burial Depth
❌ Problem: Underground wiring too shallow
   Why it fails: Safety (protection from digging damage)
   Where: Outdoor circuits, detached structures, landscape lighting
   How to fix: Re-trench and bury to proper depth
   Reference: 2022 CEC 300.5(A)
   Cost to fix: $200-800 (excavation + reburial)
   Prevention: UF cable = 24" deep, PVC conduit = 18" deep

VIOLATION 30: Indoor Romex Buried Underground
❌ Problem: NM (Romex) cable buried instead of UF
   Why it fails: NM not rated for wet/underground locations
   Where: Underground outdoor circuits
   How to fix: Replace with UF cable or THWN in conduit
   Reference: 2022 CEC 300.5(B)
   Cost to fix: $400-1,500 (excavate + replace)
   Prevention: Use UF cable or conduit for underground

CATEGORY 9: SWITCH & CLEARANCE VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 31: Switch Within Reach of Tub/Shower
❌ Problem: Light switch accessible from tub or shower
   Why it fails: Electrocution hazard when wet
   Where: Bathrooms
   How to fix: Relocate switch 5+ feet from tub/shower
   Reference: 2022 CEC 404.4(C)
   Cost to fix: $100-250
   Prevention: Keep switches away from water sources

VIOLATION 32: No Lighting with Wall Switch in Garage
❌ Problem: Garage has no light fixture OR no wall switch
   Why it fails: Code requires lighting controlled by wall switch
   Where: Garages, carports
   How to fix: Install light fixture + switch at entry
   Reference: 2022 CEC 210.70(A)(2)(a)
   Cost to fix: $100-250
   Prevention: Include switched lighting in garage plans

CATEGORY 10: SOLAR PV SPECIFIC VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 33: Missing Solar Labels/Placards
❌ Problem: Required labels not present or incorrect
   Why it fails: Code requires specific safety labeling
   Where: Solar installations (very common)
   How to fix: Install all required labels with correct specs
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.56
   Cost to fix: $50-150
   Prevention: Use pre-made label kits with correct information

VIOLATION 34: Solar 120% Rule Violated
❌ Problem: Main + solar breakers exceed 120% of bus bar
   Why it fails: Panel overload risk
   Where: Solar grid-tie interconnection
   How to fix: Service upgrade OR reduce system OR line-side tap
   Reference: 2022 CEC 705.12(B)(2)
   Cost to fix: $2,000-5,000 (if service upgrade)
   Prevention: Calculate before installing: (Main + Solar) ≤ 120% × Bus bar

VIOLATION 35: DC Disconnect Not Accessible
❌ Problem: DC disconnect switch blocked or too high
   Why it fails: Must be accessible for safety
   Where: Solar installations
   How to fix: Relocate disconnect or remove obstruction
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.15
   Cost to fix: $100-400
   Prevention: Plan disconnect locations for easy access

VIOLATION 36: Solar Conduit Overfilled
❌ Problem: Too many wires in conduit (exceeds 40%)
   Why it fails: Heat buildup, wire damage risk
   Where: Solar DC and AC conduits
   How to fix: Install larger conduit or reduce wire count
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.31, Table 1
   Cost to fix: $200-600
   Prevention: Calculate conduit fill percentage

CATEGORY 11: POOL/SPA VIOLATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

VIOLATION 37: No Class A GFCI on Pool Circuits
❌ Problem: Standard 30mA GFCI used instead of 5mA Class A
   Why it fails: Pools require more sensitive GFCI
   Where: Swimming pools, spas
   How to fix: Install Class A GFCI (5mA trip)
   Reference: 2022 CEC 680.5(B)
   Cost to fix: $100-300
   Prevention: Pools need special Class A GFCI protection

VIOLATION 38: Pool Equipment Not Bonded
❌ Problem: Pool pump, light, etc. not bonded together
   Why it fails: Creates shock hazard
   Where: Pool/spa installations
   How to fix: Install bonding conductor connecting all equipment
   Reference: 2022 CEC 680.26
   Cost to fix: $200-500
   Prevention: Bond ALL pool equipment together

VIOLATION 39: Grounding to Concrete Instead of Earth
❌ Problem: Ground rod in concrete pad instead of soil
   Why it fails: Inadequate earth ground
   Where: Pool areas, outdoor equipment
