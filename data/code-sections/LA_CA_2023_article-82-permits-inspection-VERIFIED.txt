Q1: "How long is my electrical permit valid?"
A: "12 months from issue date to START work (Section 82-2, verified). Work

Q2: "What electrical work doesn't need a permit?"

permits. BUT must still meet code - if code requires GFCI, replacement must 
be GFCI."

A: "Request extension BEFORE expiration: Submit written request, pay fee (up 
to 25% of original permit), explain circumstances. Extension granted: 180 days. 
Can request multiple extensions. After all expire: Apply for new permit."

SECTION 82-2: TIME LIMIT (✅ VERIFIED ACCURACY)
================================================================================

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Every permit issued by the Building Official under the provisions of this Code 
shall expire by limitation and become null and void if the work authorized by 
such permit is not commenced within 12 months from the date such permit is 
issued, or the work authorized by such permit is suspended or abandoned for a 
period of 180 days, or the permittee fails to obtain inspection as required by 
the provisions of Section 82-14 of this Code for a period of 180 days."

PRACTICAL_MEANING (✅ VERIFIED FROM OFFICIAL PDF):
✅ Permit valid 12 MONTHS from issue date (NOT 6 months!)
✅ Work must be STARTED within 12 months
✅ Work cannot be suspended/abandoned for more than 180 days
✅ Inspection must be obtained within 180 days of commencement

SECTION 82-3: WORK REQUIRING A PERMIT (9 EXEMPTIONS)
================================================================================

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"No person shall install, alter, reconstruct or repair any electrical wiring, 
devices, appliances, apparatus, or equipment, within or on any building, 
structure or premises without first obtaining a permit therefor from the Chief 
Electrical Inspector, except as follows:"

PRACTICAL_MEANING: ONLY these 9 specific types of work are exempt from permits
Everything else requires a permit!

🚨 DEFAULT RULE: ALL electrical work requires permit
    EXCEPTIONS: Only these 9 categories below
    WHEN IN DOUBT: PULL PERMIT

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(a) MINOR REPAIR WORK - OUTLET/SWITCH REPLACEMENT

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Minor repair work, such as the replacement of lamps, switches, receptacle 
devices, sockets, and the like, or the connection of portable motor and 
appliances to suitable receptacles which have been permanently installed."

✅ EXEMPT (Minor repair):
→ Replacing light bulbs
→ Replacing outlet covers/wall plates
→ Replacing electrical switches (same type/function)
→ Replacing electrical receptacles (same type/rating)
→ Plugging portable equipment into existing outlets

✗ NOT EXEMPT:
→ Adding new circuits
→ Adding new outlets or switches
→ Rewiring existing circuits
→ Changing outlet/switch locations
→ Upgrading to different outlet types

⚡ CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP:
Replacing existing = Likely exempt (same location, same type)
Adding new = PERMIT REQUIRED
When in doubt = Pull permit

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(b) TEMPORARY STAGE WIRING

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"The wiring for temporary theater, motion picture or television stage sets."

PRACTICAL: Temporary wiring for productions, removed after use
✓ EXEMPT: Temporary stage lighting/power for film shoot (removed after)
✗ NOT EXEMPT: Permanent theater lighting installation

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(c) LOW-VOLTAGE SYSTEMS (<25V, <50W)

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Electrical wiring, devices, appliances, apparatus, or equipment operating at 
less than 25 volts and not capable of supplying more than 50 watts of energy."

BOTH CONDITIONS MUST BE MET:
1. Voltage: Less than 25 volts
2. Power: Not more than 50 watts

✅ EXEMPT: Doorbell (24V, <50W), thermostat circuits, low-voltage landscape lighting
✗ NOT EXEMPT: Normal household circuits (120V/240V), systems >50W

HOW TO CHECK:
1. Find voltage rating (V)
2. Find current rating (A)
3. Calculate: Watts = Volts × Amps
4. Both <25V AND <50W? = EXEMPT

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(d) LOW-ENERGY CIRCUITS (<30V, <1000VA)

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Low-energy power, control, and signal circuits that are not an integral part 
of an appliance and in which the power is limited from a source having a rated 
output of not more than 30 volts and 1,000 volt-amperes."

PRACTICAL: Private communication systems, security alarms, intercom systems

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(e) TEMPORARY DECORATIVE LIGHTING

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Temporary decorative lighting."

✅ EXEMPT: Holiday lights, event lighting, seasonal displays
✗ NOT EXEMPT: Permanent outdoor lighting, year-round lighting

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(f) TEMPORARY TESTING WIRING

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"The installation of temporary wiring for testing or experimental purposes 
within suitable facilities."

PRACTICAL: Laboratory testing, experimental electrical installations (temporary)

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(g) OVERCURRENT DEVICE REPLACEMENT

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Replacement of over-current devices of the same type and the same rating."

BOTH CONDITIONS MUST BE MET:
1. Same TYPE (standard, AFCI, GFCI, etc.)
2. Same RATING (amperage)

✅ EXEMPT: 20A standard → 20A standard breaker
✗ NOT EXEMPT: 15A → 20A breaker OR standard → AFCI breaker

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(h) PORTABLE EQUIPMENT

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Portable generators, portable motors, appliances, tools, power outlets, and 
other portable equipment connected by means of a cord or cable having an 
attachment plug."

✅ EXEMPT: Portable generator, power tools, space heater with plug
✗ NOT EXEMPT: Permanently installed equipment, hardwired generators

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

(i) PRIVATE COMMUNICATIONS SYSTEMS

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Private telephone, intercom, sound and communication systems; provided, however, 
that the above system(s) do not exceed the value as indicated in (c) and (d) of 
this section. A permit shall be obtained for the power supplies required by the 
above systems."

🚨 CRITICAL: Systems may be exempt IF meeting (c) & (d) voltage/wattage limits
BUT power supplies ALWAYS require permits!

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

UNIVERSAL COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENT:

OFFICIAL TEXT:
"Notwithstanding the foregoing, all electrical wiring and equipment shall 
comply with Section 83-2 of this Code."

🚨 CRITICAL UNDERSTANDING:
EXEMPT FROM PERMIT ≠ EXEMPT FROM CODE COMPLIANCE

Example: Replace outlet without permit (exempt)
BUT must still be GFCI if code requires GFCI at that location

COMPREHENSIVE RAG QUERY EXAMPLES
================================================================================

Q: "What electrical code does LA County use?"
A: "LA County adopts 2022 California Electrical Code (CEC) by reference (Section 
80-1.5). All 2022 CEC technical requirements apply, plus LA County administrative 
procedures (Title 27 Article 82). LA County amendments override CEC when more 
restrictive (Section 82-2, verified)."

Q: "Do I need a permit for electrical work in LA County?"
A: "Yes, permit required for almost all electrical work (Section 82-1). Only 9 
specific exemptions (Section 82-3): minor repairs, temporary stage, low-voltage 
<25V/<50W, low-energy <30V/<1000VA, temporary decorative, temporary testing, 
breaker replacement same type/rating, portable equipment, private communications 
(if meeting voltage limits). Everything else requires permit. Apply: epicla.lacounty.gov"

Q: "How long is my electrical permit valid?"
A: "12 months from issue date to START work (Section 82-2, verified against 
official LA County PDF). NOT 6 months - this is most common misconception. Work 
cannot be suspended more than 180 consecutive days. Inspection must be requested 
within 180 days of starting work. Extensions available: 180 days each, fee up 
to 25% of original permit."

Q: "Who can pull electrical permits in LA County?"
A: "For work over $200: Licensed C-10 contractor, registered maintenance 
electrician (employer's property only), homeowner (own principal residence only, 
must do work themselves), or government representative (Section 82-4). For work 
under $200: Contact LA County for clarification (626) 458-3180."

Q: "Can I replace an outlet without a permit?"
A: "Outlet replacement (same type/rating, same location) is minor repair exempt 
from permits (Section 82-3(a)). However, must still meet all code requirements 
(Section 83-2). If code requires GFCI at that location, replacement outlet must 
be GFCI even though permit not required."

Q: "What happens if my permit expires?"
A: "Request extension BEFORE expiration: Submit written request to Building 
Official via EPIC-LA, explain circumstances causing delay, pay extension fee 
(up to 25% of original permit). Extension granted: 180 days. Can request 
multiple extensions (no limit specified in code). After all extensions expire: 
Must file new permit with full new fees."

Q: "Can homeowner do their own electrical work?"
A: "Homeowner can pull own permit for principal residence (where they live, 
single-family only) IF they do the work themselves or with immediate family 
members only. Homeowner becomes legal general contractor with SIGNIFICANT legal 
responsibilities: tax withholding, workers' compensation insurance if hiring any 
help, full legal liability. See Section 82-4(c) refinements for full details."

Q: "What is immediate family for homeowner permits?"
A: "LA County Code does not explicitly define 'immediate family.' Typical 
interpretation: spouse, children, parents. Other relationships: Get written 
clarification from Chief Electrical Inspector FIRST at (626) 458-3180. Don't 
assume - violating this can result in immediate permit cancellation."

Q: "When can inspector do final inspection?"
A: "Must request inspection at least 24 hours in advance (minimum requirement) 
via EPIC-LA. Actual inspection date depends on inspector workload - not guaranteed 
by county. Typical: 1-4 weeks. Summer (busy): 3-4 weeks. Winter (slower): 1-2 
weeks. Request immediately after completing work (don't delay)."

Q: "Can I hire a friend to help with electrical work under homeowner permit?"
A: "No. If homeowner pulls permit, homeowner or immediate family must do ALL 
work. Cannot hire unlicensed friends, neighbors, or handymen. If hiring ANY 
workers: Contractor must pull permit (not homeowner), and you must handle tax 
withholding and workers' compensation insurance. Hiring unlicensed worker = 
immediate permit cancellation."

COMPREHENSIVE RAG QUERY EXAMPLES
================================================================================

Q: "What electrical code does LA County use?"
A: "LA County adopts 2022 California Electrical Code (CEC) by reference (Section 
80-1.5). All 2022 CEC technical requirements apply, plus LA County administrative 
procedures (Title 27 Article 82). LA County amendments override CEC when more 
restrictive (Section 82-2, verified)."

Q: "Do I need a permit for electrical work in LA County?"
A: "Yes, permit required for almost all electrical work (Section 82-1). Only 9 
specific exemptions (Section 82-3): minor repairs, temporary stage, low-voltage 
<25V/<50W, low-energy <30V/<1000VA, temporary decorative, temporary testing, 
breaker replacement same type/rating, portable equipment, private communications 
(if meeting voltage limits). Everything else requires permit. Apply: epicla.lacounty.gov"

Q: "How long is my electrical permit valid?"
A: "12 months from issue date to START work (Section 82-2, verified against 
official LA County PDF). NOT 6 months - this is most common misconception. Work 
cannot be suspended more than 180 consecutive days. Inspection must be requested 
within 180 days of starting work. Extensions available: 180 days each, fee up 
to 25% of original permit."

Q: "Who can pull electrical permits in LA County?"
A: "For work over $200: Licensed C-10 contractor, registered maintenance 
electrician (employer's property only), homeowner (own principal residence only, 
must do work themselves), or government representative (Section 82-4). For work 
under $200: Contact LA County for clarification (626) 458-3180."

Q: "Can I replace an outlet without a permit?"
A: "Outlet replacement (same type/rating, same location) is minor repair exempt 
from permits (Section 82-3(a)). However, must still meet all code requirements 
(Section 83-2). If code requires GFCI at that location, replacement outlet must 
be GFCI even though permit not required."

Q: "What happens if my permit expires?"
A: "Request extension BEFORE expiration: Submit written request to Building 
Official via EPIC-LA, explain circumstances causing delay, pay extension fee 
(up to 25% of original permit). Extension granted: 180 days. Can request 
multiple extensions (no limit specified in code). After all extensions expire: 
Must file new permit with full new fees."

Q: "Can homeowner do their own electrical work?"
A: "Homeowner can pull own permit for principal residence (where they live, 
single-family only) IF they do the work themselves or with immediate family 
members only. Homeowner becomes legal general contractor with SIGNIFICANT legal 
responsibilities: tax withholding, workers' compensation insurance if hiring any 
help, full legal liability. See Section 82-4(c) refinements for full details."

Q: "What is immediate family for homeowner permits?"
A: "LA County Code does not explicitly define 'immediate family.' Typical 
interpretation: spouse, children, parents. Other relationships: Get written 
clarification from Chief Electrical Inspector FIRST at (626) 458-3180. Don't 
assume - violating this can result in immediate permit cancellation."

Q: "When can inspector do final inspection?"
A: "Must request inspection at least 24 hours in advance (minimum requirement) 
via EPIC-LA. Actual inspection date depends on inspector workload - not guaranteed 
by county. Typical: 1-4 weeks. Summer (busy): 3-4 weeks. Winter (slower): 1-2 
weeks. Request immediately after completing work (don't delay)."

Q: "Can I hire a friend to help with electrical work under homeowner permit?"
A: "No. If homeowner pulls permit, homeowner or immediate family must do ALL 
work. Cannot hire unlicensed friends, neighbors, or handymen. If hiring ANY 
workers: Contractor must pull permit (not homeowner), and you must handle tax 
withholding and workers' compensation insurance. Hiring unlicensed worker = 
immediate permit cancellation."
