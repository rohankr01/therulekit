💼 CONTRACTOR ESSENTIALS - PROCESS MANAGEMENT:

✅ APPLICATION METHOD: EPIC-LA ONLY (epicla.lacounty.gov - mandatory)
✅ APPROVAL TIMELINE: 2-5 days (simple), 5-15 days (complex)
✅ INSPECTION SCHEDULING: EPIC-LA ONLY (online scheduling mandatory)
✅ INSPECTION WAIT TIME: Summer 2-3 weeks, Winter 1-2 weeks
✅ PERMIT VALIDITY: 12 months to START work (not 6 months!)
✅ ROUGH-IN INSPECTION: BEFORE drywall/covering (non-negotiable)
✅ FINAL INSPECTION: AFTER all work complete + GFCI testing
✅ VIRTUAL INSPECTIONS: Available for some types (check EPIC-LA)
✅ EMERGENCY SERVICE: 1-800-675-4357 (24/7, safety hazards only)

🚨 CRITICAL CONTRACTOR ALERTS:

⚠️ EPIC-LA IS MANDATORY (No Phone Scheduling):
   All applications submitted through EPIC-LA ONLY
   All inspections scheduled through EPIC-LA ONLY
   Calling to schedule = REJECTED (must use online)
   Real-time tracking shows inspector assignments and timing
   Email confirmations automatic (saves phone calls)

⚠️ SEASONAL PLANNING CRITICAL:
   Summer (May-Sept): 2-3 week inspection waits
   Winter (Nov-Feb): 1-2 week inspection waits
   Spring (Mar-Apr): 2 week waits (moderate busy)
   Fall (Sept-Oct): 1-2 week waits (slower)
   Plan project timelines accordingly
   Summer projects: Add +3 weeks to schedule

⚠️ ROUGH-IN TIMING NON-NEGOTIABLE:
   Must schedule rough-in BEFORE drywall/covering starts
   Inspector must see all wiring, boxes, grounding visibly
   Covering work before inspection = WALLS MUST BE OPENED
   Cost: Contractor pays for wall opening/reclosing
   Solution: Coordinate rough-in inspection BEFORE drywall crew

⚠️ GFCI TESTING REQUIRED BEFORE FINAL:
   Test ALL GFCI receptacles before final inspection
   Inspector will test during final - failures = re-inspection
   Simple button test (15 seconds per outlet) prevents failures
   Bring testing device to job site
   Document all tests in job log

⚠️ PERMIT VALIDITY IS 12 MONTHS (Not 6 months!):
   Most common mistake: "Permits expire in 6 months"
   Actual rule: 12 months to START work (Section 82-2)
   But: Work cannot be suspended >180 consecutive days
   Mark calendar: 12 months from permit issue date
   Plan accordingly for job start

⚠️ PRE-INSPECTION FIELD PREPARATION CRITICAL:
   Inspector expects professional job site
   Disorganized site = Inspector frustration
   Bad appearance = Closer inspection = More violations found
   Professional prep = Faster inspections
   Time spent preparing = Time saved on inspection

🎯 TOP 5 ELECTRICIAN FIELD QUESTIONS ANSWERED:

Q1: "How long does the WHOLE permit process take?"
A: "3-12 weeks depending on complexity and season.
   Simple winter project (1,200 sq ft house): 3-4 weeks
   Typical spring project (2,000 sq ft house): 5-7 weeks
   Complex summer project (service upgrade): 8-12 weeks
   Key variable: Inspection wait times (2-3 weeks typical)"

Q2: "Can I schedule inspection by phone or email?"
A: "NO! EPIC-LA only. Call or email = REJECTED
   Why? System ensures fair scheduling and prevents favoritism
   Online = Real-time availability + instant confirmation
   Calling = Added to phone queue = Slower response
   Inspector assignment emailed automatically = Professional"

Q3: "What's the difference between rough-in and final?"
A: "ROUGH-IN: Before drywall/covering
   • Inspector checks: Wiring, boxes, grounding, service entrance
   • What's visible: All circuits, all connections
   • Time: ~30-45 minutes
   • Required: YES (mandatory for most work)

   FINAL: After all work complete
   • Inspector checks: Fixtures, outlets, GFCI test, panel labels
   • What's tested: GFCI operation, grounding continuity
   • Time: ~45-60 minutes
   • Required: YES (mandatory, always needed)"

Q4: "What if inspector fails my work?"
A: "Inspector sends correction list via EPIC-LA email
   • Each violation clearly described
   • Location of problem noted
   • Code section referenced
   • Correction method explained
   
   Your response:
   1. Fix all violations (don't argue on site)
   2. Verify fixes meet code
   3. Request re-inspection through EPIC-LA
   4. Usually NO RE-INSPECTION FEE for corrections
   5. Inspector returns within 1-2 weeks
   
   If disagree with violation:
   • Don't argue with inspector on site
   • Submit formal appeal to Chief Inspector
   • (626) 458-3180 for appeal process"

Q5: "Can I start work before permit approval?"
A: "NO! ABSOLUTELY NOT!
   Consequences:
   • Stop-work order
   • Fines and penalties
   • May require removal of all work done
   • Electrical service can be disconnected
   • Insurance may deny coverage
   
   Wait for EPIC-LA approval email FIRST
   Keep printed permit on-site for inspector verification"

STEP 1: VERIFY JURISDICTION - BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PURPOSE: Confirm you're in LA County (not city) jurisdiction

WHY THIS MATTERS:
• Wrong jurisdiction = Wrong code = Wrong permits
• Some areas look like LA County but are actually cities
• City codes different from LA County
• Using wrong code = Time wasted + Customer frustration

VERIFICATION PROCESS:

ONLINE (Recommended - Takes 2 minutes):
1. Go to: permits.lacounty.gov
2. Enter: Complete street address
3. Result shows: LA County Public Works OR City name
4. If "LA County Public Works" → Use this guide
5. If city name shown → Use that city's permitting process

PHONE VERIFICATION (Takes 5 minutes):
📞 Call: (626) 458-3180
Ask: "I'm doing electrical work at [address]. Is this LA County jurisdiction 
or a city? What code applies?"

COMMON MISTAKES - JURISDICTION:
❌ Assuming all LA County area = LA County jurisdiction (WRONG)
   Many incorporated cities are WITHIN LA County geographically
   But they use their own codes, not LA County codes
   
Examples of INCORPORATED CITIES (Not LA County):
   • City of Los Angeles
   • Santa Monica
   • Pasadena
   • Long Beach
   • Glendale
   • West Hollywood
   • Torrance
   • Compton
   (And 60+ others)

⚡ CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP:
• Verify EVERY project with permits.lacounty.gov
• Don't assume based on mailing address
• 2 minutes verification = Prevents major errors
• Bookmark: permits.lacounty.gov in browser

🚨 FIELD MISTAKE:
Assuming all LA County projects = Same code
Result: Wrong permit type, wrong fees, wrong inspections

STEP 7: SCHEDULE ROUGH-IN INSPECTION - (Before drywall!)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CRITICAL TIMING: Schedule BEFORE drywall/covering starts!

WHY TIMING MATTERS:
• Inspector needs to see all wiring, boxes, grounding VISIBLE
• After drywall = Inspector can't verify work
• After covering = You must open walls for inspection
• Opening walls = High cost + Customer frustration
• Solution: Schedule rough-in BEFORE covering

WHEN TO SCHEDULE:
Timeline:
• Monday: Wiring complete, boxes installed, grounding done
• Tuesday: Ready for inspection (before drywall Wednesday)
• Tuesday morning: Schedule rough-in for Wednesday/Thursday
• Wednesday or Thursday: Inspection occurs
• Friday: Continue to drywall if inspection passes

SCHEDULING PROCESS - EPIC-LA:

STEP 7A: Log into EPIC-LA
→ Website: epicla.lacounty.gov
→ Go to: "My Permits" → "My Approved Permit"

STEP 7B: Select "Schedule Inspection"
→ Click: "Request Rough-In Inspection" (or similar button)
→ Choose: Inspection type (Rough-In for electrical)
→ Enter: Preferred dates/times

STEP 7C: Choose Date/Time
EPIC-LA shows:
• Available inspection slots (next 2-4 weeks)
• Time windows (e.g., "8 AM - 12 PM" or "1 PM - 5 PM")
• Season-specific wait times displayed

Selection strategy:
✓ BEST: Winter (1-2 week wait)
✓ GOOD: Spring/Fall (2 weeks)
✓ CHALLENGING: Summer (3-4 week wait)

Choose earliest available to lock in date

STEP 7D: Confirm & Submit
→ Verify date/time correct
→ Click: "Confirm Inspection Request"
→ Status changes: "Inspection Scheduled"

CONFIRMATION:
• Confirmation email sent (immediately)
• Inspection date/time displayed in EPIC-LA
• Inspector assigned (may or may not be named)
• Location/contact information shown

⚡ CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP:
• Schedule immediately when work is almost ready (not after drywall!)
• Request early morning if possible (morning slots = more flexible)
• Add buffer: Reschedule gives you flexibility
• Put inspection date on job timeline (shared with crew)

🚨 FIELD MISTAKES:
Mistake 1: Scheduling AFTER drywall = Inspector can't see work
Mistake 2: Scheduling too late = Drywall crew already booked
Mistake 3: Scheduling AM but work not ready until PM = Rescheduling needed

ROUGH-IN INSPECTION PROCESS (Typical 30-45 minutes):

INSPECTOR ACTIONS:
1. Arrives at site with printed permit
2. Looks for permit display (must be visible)
3. Asks for contractor/supervisor
4. Reviews work scope on permit
5. Visually inspects all electrical work:
   • Wiring runs and support
   • Outlet/switch box placement
   • Service entrance installation
   • Grounding/bonding
   • Panel placement
6. May use testing equipment to verify:
   • Continuity (grounding checks)
   • Voltage checks (if power on)
7. Asks questions about installation:
   • Why done this way?
   • Wire gauge selected?
   • Grounding method?
8. Takes notes on violations (if any)
9. Either approves or cites violations

POSSIBLE OUTCOMES:

✅ PASS (Approved):
• Inspector approves rough-in on-site or via email
• Status in EPIC-LA: "Rough-In Approved"
• Can proceed to next phase (drywall, cover-up)
• Now can schedule final inspection
• Usual: Same day or next day notification

❌ VIOLATION(S) NOTED:
• Inspector lists violations (code sections cited)
• Violations emailed via EPIC-LA
• Examples: 
  - Wire not properly supported
  - Box height incorrect
  - Grounding inadequate
  - Labeling unclear
• You must fix violations
• Schedule re-inspection after corrections
• Usually no additional fee for re-inspection

🤔 CONDITIONAL APPROVAL:
• Inspector approves with conditions
• Examples: 
  - Fix labeling before cover-up
  - Add support straps before drywall
  - Correct one outlet box height
• Must complete conditions before cover-up
• Inspector may return to verify corrections

WHAT INSPECTOR WILL ASK:

Typical inspector questions:
• "Who performed this work?" (Verify licensed)
• "Why is this wire size?" (Verify code calculation)
• "Where's your grounding?" (Verify adequate)
• "These circuits - what loads?" (Verify sizing)
• "When will drywall cover this?" (Project timeline)
• "Any other electrical on job?" (Hidden work check)

⚡ CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP:
Be prepared to answer questions. Have answers:
✓ "20 gauge wire here because 15A circuit, code requires minimum 14 gauge, 
  using 12 gauge for future-proofing"
✗ "I dunno, that's what came in the box"

Informed answers = Inspector confidence = Faster approval

🚨 FIELD MISTAKES - INSPECTION DAY:

Mistake 1: Contractor not on site
→ Inspector can't answer questions
→ Inspection delayed or incomplete

Mistake 2: Arguing with inspector about violation
→ Inspector is enforcing code (not opinion)
→ Arguing = Inspection delays + more scrutiny
→ Accept violation, fix, re-inspect

Mistake 3: Covering work before rough-in pass
→ Code violation (covers wiring)
→ Inspector may require wall opening
→ Your cost to fix

Mistake 4: Multiple issues (wiring + grounding + labeling)
→ Inspector cites all violations
→ Longer fix time
→ Delayed rework

Mistake 5: Being unprepared or dismissive
→ Inspector frustrated
→ Takes more scrutiny to next violation
→ Longer inspection time

⚡ PRE-FINAL CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP - GFCI TESTING:

GFCI failures = Most common final inspection failure
Spend 20 minutes testing all GFCI:

For each GFCI outlet:
1. Press TEST button (should kill power immediately)
2. If no power: GOOD
3. Press RESET button (power should return)
4. If power returns: GOOD
5. If either fails: REPLACE immediately

For each GFCI breaker:
1. Same process - TEST, then RESET

Inspector will do this test too
If GFCI fails during inspector's test = Failure
If you replace before inspection = Pass

20-minute prep = Avoids 3-week re-inspection delay

SCHEDULING FINAL INSPECTION - Via EPIC-LA:

TIMING: After all work complete

1. Log into EPIC-LA
2. Go to: "My Permits" → Approved permit
3. Click: "Request Final Inspection"
4. Choose: Available dates/times
5. Select: Earliest available (lock in date)
6. Confirm & submit

WAIT TIMES:
• Winter: 1-2 weeks
• Spring/Fall: 2 weeks
• Summer: 2-3 weeks

FINAL INSPECTION DAY - What to Expect:

Inspector arrives (typically morning)

FINAL INSPECTION PROCESS (Typical 45 minutes - 1 hour):

1. VERIFICATION:
   • Permit displayed? (Must be visible)
   • Work matches scope on permit?
   • Correct address/location?

2. FIXTURE INSPECTION:
   • All fixtures installed?
   • Fixtures properly secured?
   • Devices properly mounted?
   • No damage/defects?

3. GFCI TESTING (Critical):
   • Inspector tests ALL GFCI outlets
   • Inspector tests ALL GFCI breakers
   • Must TEST and RESET function properly
   • All must pass (failures = re-inspection)

4. GROUNDING VERIFICATION:
   • Continuity testing if needed
   • Bonding verification
   • Ground rod resistance check (if applicable)

5. PANEL/SERVICE VERIFICATION:
   • Breaker sizing correct?
   • Labeling accurate?
   • Main disconnect functional?
   • All breakers properly installed?

6. OPERATIONAL TEST:
   • Inspector tests circuits (lights, outlets work?)
   • All switches functional?
   • No unusual sounds/smells?
   • No loose connections?

POSSIBLE OUTCOMES:

✅ FINAL APPROVAL (Pass):
• Inspector approves on-site or via email (usually same day)
• Status in EPIC-LA: "Final Approved"
• Work is COMPLETE and APPROVED
• Certificate of Compliance issued (if required)
• Payment process complete
• Work can be energized if not already

❌ VIOLATIONS NOTED:
• Inspector cites violations (code sections referenced)
• Email sent via EPIC-LA

  - GFCI not functioning
  - Loose connections
  - Missing labels
  - Improper grounding
• You must fix violations
• Request re-inspection (usually same fee structure)
• Typically 3-5 day re-inspection after fixes

• Approval with minor corrections required

• Must complete before occupancy
• Inspector may do walk-through verification

STEP 12: HANDLING INSPECTION FAILURES & VIOLATIONS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

IF INSPECTION FAILS - Don't Panic

UNDERSTANDING INSPECTION FAILURES:

Inspector's role: Verify code compliance (not opinion)
• Violations are specific code sections
• Not personal attacks on work
• Code-based, not arbitrary
• Process is professional, not emotional

WHEN VIOLATION OCCURS:

IMMEDIATE (On-site):
1. Inspector shows violation clearly
2. Inspector cites specific code section (write it down)
3. Inspector may show on-site solution
4. Don't argue on-site (won't change outcome)
5. Accept violation professionally

WITHIN 24 HOURS:
→ EPIC-LA sends violation email
→ Lists each violation clearly:
   • Violation description
   • Code section cited
   • Correction method explained
   • Location in property
   • Inspector contact info

VIOLATION EXAMPLES:

EXAMPLE 1: GFCI Failure
Violation: "GFCI outlet tested, failed - not providing protection"
Code: "CEC Article 210.8 - GFCI required at kitchen sink"
Fix: "Replace with functioning GFCI outlet"
Your action: Replace outlet, test, re-inspect

EXAMPLE 2: Loose Connection
Violation: "Grounding continuity failed at panel - loose connection"
Code: "CEC Article 250 - Proper bonding required"
Fix: "Tighten ground connection, verify continuity"
Your action: Fix, test, re-inspect

EXAMPLE 3: Missing Label
Violation: "Circuit not labeled - cannot verify function"
Code: "LA County Code 82-X - All circuits must be labeled"
Fix: "Label all circuits clearly with amperage and purpose"
Your action: Label panel, take photo, re-inspect

RESPONSE PROCESS:

STEP 1: Receive Email Violation Notice
→ EPIC-LA emails violation details
→ Keep email (document for records)

STEP 2: Analyze Violation
→ Read code section cited
→ Understand requirement
→ Determine fix needed

STEP 3: Fix Violation
→ Perform work to correct issue
→ Test correction (if testable)
→ Verify code compliance

STEP 4: Request Re-Inspection
→ Log into EPIC-LA
→ Select: "Request Re-Inspection"
→ Choose: Available date/time
→ Submit

STEP 5: Re-Inspection
→ Inspector verifies correction
→ Usually no additional fee
→ Either approves or cites new issues

STEP 6: Receive Approval
→ If all corrections pass = Approved
→ EPIC-LA status updates to "Approved"
→ Work complete

⚡ CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP:
Fix violations PROMPTLY:
✓ Fix same day if possible
✓ Request re-inspection next day
✓ Keeps project momentum
✓ Avoids scheduling delays

🚨 MISTAKES HANDLING VIOLATIONS:

Mistake 1: Arguing with inspector on-site
→ Won't change outcome
→ Creates negative record
→ May invite more scrutiny
→ Use appeal process if disagree

Mistake 2: Delaying fixes
→ Every day delayed = Inspection wait adds
→ Summer = 3-week wait = Project stopped
→ Fix immediately

Mistake 3: Not understanding violation
→ Read the code section cited
→ Ask someone who knows code
→ Don't re-inspect without understanding

Mistake 4: Same mistake on re-inspection
→ Inspector loses confidence
→ More scrutiny on final inspection
→ Take time to fix properly

APPEALING A VIOLATION (If You Disagree):

IF YOU BELIEVE VIOLATION IS WRONG:

Process:
1. Don't argue on-site
2. Document everything (photos, measurements, code section)
3. Contact Chief Inspector: (626) 458-3180
4. Request: Formal appeal of violation
5. Provide: Technical justification for your position
6. Wait: 5-10 business days for response
7. Result: Chief Inspector upholds or overturns violation

Important: Appeals don't prevent re-inspection
• Appeal is formal process
• Takes time (10+ days)
• Doesn't move project forward
• Usually better to fix and move forward

STEP 13: EMERGENCY SAFETY HAZARDS - When to Contact Dispatch
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHAT CONSTITUTES "EMERGENCY ELECTRICAL HAZARD":

Emergency situations (call 1-800-675-4357):
🚨 Active electrical fire (flames, smoking)
🚨 Exposed live wires (actively energized)
🚨 Downed power lines (any size)
🚨 Electrocution risk (water + electricity)
🚨 Unsafe installation causing immediate danger
🚨 Service disconnected for safety violations
🚨 Customer safety hazard (exposed live parts)

Non-emergency situations (use EPIC-LA):
⏱️ Code questions
⏱️ Permit process questions
⏱️ Inspection scheduling
⏱️ Violation clarifications
⏱️ Project timeline questions

EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROCESS:

IF EMERGENCY DISCOVERED:

1. IMMEDIATE SAFETY:
   → Stop all work immediately
   → Clear area of people
   → If fire: Call 911 FIRST
   → If electrocution risk: Don't touch, call 911

2. CALL DISPATCH:
   📞 Phone: 1-800-675-4357
   Say: "Electrical safety emergency at [address]"
   Provide: Location, nature of hazard, your contact info

3. EXPECTED RESPONSE:
   → 24-48 hour response typical
   → Inspector dispatched to investigate
   → Safety hazard corrected or work stopped

4. FOLLOW-UP:
   → Inspector will contact you
   → May require work stoppage
   → Safety corrections required
   → Resume work only after hazard corrected

COMMON EMERGENCY SCENARIOS:

SCENARIO 1: Electrical Fire
Discovery: Smoking/flames from outlet or panel
Action:
1. Call 911 (fire department)
2. Evacuate building
3. Call (626) 458-3180 after safe (building electrical dept)
4. Don't attempt to fight fire
5. Wait for professionals

SCENARIO 2: Exposed Live Wires
Discovery: Uncovered energized wire (actively live)
Action:
1. Stop all work
2. Clear area (keep people away)
3. Call 1-800-675-4357
4. Don't touch or cover wire
5. Wait for inspector

SCENARIO 3: Downed Power Lines
Discovery: Power line on ground (from storm, accident, etc.)
Action:
1. Call 911 (fire department emergency)
2. Stay away (minimum 10 feet)
3. Call 1-800-675-4357 (second)
4. Don't touch line
5. Mark area
6. Wait for utility company

SCENARIO 4: Service Disconnected for Safety
Discovery: Your service has been disconnected by inspector
Action:
1. Call Building & Safety: (626) 458-3180
2. Ask: Why was service disconnected?
3. Ask: What's required to reconnect?
4. Perform required corrections
5. Call again for inspection of corrections
6. Service reconnected after approval

⚡ CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP - EMERGENCY PREP:
Keep emergency contact numbers on job site:
📋 Post at site entrance:
   911 - Emergency
   1-800-675-4357 - LA County Electrical Emergency
   (626) 458-3180 - LA County Building & Safety
   [Your contact number]

🚨 FIELD MISTAKES - EMERGENCIES:

Mistake 1: Not calling 911 for fire/electrocution
→ Life-threatening delay
→ Catastrophic consequences
→ Always call 911 first for life safety

Mistake 2: Attempting to fix emergency yourself
→ Risk of electrocution
→ Risk of fire spread
→ Risk of injury
→ Let professionals handle

Mistake 3: Not reporting safety hazard
→ Customer in danger
→ Liability for you
→ Code violation
→ Report immediately

Mistake 4: Covering up emergency hazard
→ Code violation
→ Liability exposure
→ May face criminal charges if injury results
→ Never hide safety hazards
