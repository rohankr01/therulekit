PRACTICAL GUIDE: Solar PV System Installation
================================================================================

STEP-BY-STEP SOLAR INSTALLATION PROCESS:

PHASE 1: PLANNING & DESIGN (Critical for Solar)

1. Site Assessment
   Roof evaluation:
   □ Age of roof (should have 15+ years life remaining)
   □ Structural capacity (solar adds 2-5 lbs/sq ft)
   □ Orientation (south-facing ideal, west/east acceptable)
   □ Shading analysis (trees, buildings, obstructions)
   □ Available roof area (100 sq ft per kW typical)
   
   Shading considerations:
   - Even small shade drastically reduces output
   - Morning shade less critical than afternoon shade
   - Tree trimming may be necessary
   - Consider seasonal shading patterns

2. System Sizing
   Determine system size (kW):
   
   Method 1: Based on electricity usage
   - Review past 12 months utility bills
   - Calculate average daily kWh usage
   - Size system to offset 80-100% of usage
   
   Example calculation:
   Monthly usage: 900 kWh
   Daily usage: 900 kWh ÷ 30 days = 30 kWh/day
   Solar needed: 30 kWh ÷ 5 sun hours = 6 kW system
   
   Method 2: Based on available roof space
   - Measure usable south-facing roof area
   - 100 sq ft per kW typical (modern panels)
   - Example: 600 sq ft roof = 6 kW system max
   
   Typical residential sizes:
   - Small home: 3-5 kW
   - Average home: 6-8 kW
   - Large home: 9-12 kW

3. Select Equipment
   Solar panels:
   - Efficiency: 15-22% (higher better, more expensive)
   - Wattage: 300-400W per panel typical
   - Type: Monocrystalline (best), Polycrystalline (budget)
   - Warranty: 25 years performance typical
   - Brands: SunPower, LG, Panasonic, Canadian Solar
   
   Inverter (3 types):
   
   Type 1: String Inverter (Most common, 80% of systems)
   - One central inverter for entire array
   - Lower cost ($1,000-2,500)
   - All panels perform at weakest panel level
   - Not ideal if shading present
   
   Type 2: Micro-inverters (Premium, 15% of systems)
   - One micro-inverter per panel
   - Higher cost ($2,500-4,500)
   - Each panel independent (better with shading)
   - Easier to expand system later
   - Brands: Enphase, APsystems
   
   Type 3: Power Optimizers + String Inverter (5% of systems)
   - Hybrid approach
   - Mid-range cost ($1,500-3,500)
   - Panel-level optimization with central inverter
   - Brand: SolarEdge

4. Check Service Capacity (120% Rule)
   Calculate if service upgrade needed:
   
   Formula: Main breaker + Solar breaker ≤ 120% of bus bar rating
   
   Example 1: 200A service, 8 kW solar
   - Solar breaker size: 8,000W ÷ 240V = 33.3A → Use 40A breaker
   - Check: 200A + 40A = 240A
   - Bus bar rating: 200A × 1.2 = 240A
   - Result: 240A ≤ 240A → OK! ✓
   
   Example 2: 100A service, 8 kW solar
   - Solar breaker: 40A (same as above)
   - Check: 100A + 40A = 140A
   - Bus bar rating: 100A × 1.2 = 120A
   - Result: 140A > 120A → Service upgrade needed! ✗
   
   If upgrade needed:
   - Must upgrade service before or with solar installation
   - Typical: Upgrade from 100A to 200A
   - Cost: $2,000-5,000 additional
   - See: cross-reference-service-upgrades-LA.txt

5. Obtain Utility Approval (Critical Step!)
   Before installation:
   □ Contact utility company (SCE, LADWP, etc.)
   □ Submit interconnection application
   □ Utility reviews system design
   □ Utility approval required before proceeding
   □ Process: 2-8 weeks typical
   
   Utility requirements:
   - System design review
   - Net metering agreement
   - Interconnection agreement
   - Special metering equipment (bidirectional meter)
   
   Net metering:
   - Sell excess solar production to utility
   - Get credits on electricity bill
   - Rates vary by utility and time-of-use
   - Critical for economics of solar

PHASE 2: PERMIT APPLICATION (Multiple Permits Required)

1. Electrical Permit (LA County)
   Apply via EPIC-LA:
   URL: epicla.lacounty.gov
   Permit type: "Solar Photovoltaic System - Electrical"
   
   Required documents:
   □ Site plan (roof layout showing panels)
   □ Single-line electrical diagram
   □ Three-line electrical diagram
   □ Equipment specifications (panels, inverter)
   □ Interconnection diagram
   □ Load calculations
   □ Structural calculations (roof load)
   □ Professional engineer stamp (usually required)
   □ Utility interconnection approval
   □ Contractor license # (C-10 or C-46)
   
   Applicant requirements:
   - Licensed C-10 (General Electrical) OR
   - Licensed C-46 (Solar) contractor
   - Both can pull electrical permits for solar

2. Building Permit (if required)
   May need separate building permit for:
   □ Roof penetrations (mounting)
   □ Structural modifications
   □ Ground-mounted systems
   
   Check with LA County if building permit also needed

3. Pay Permit Fees
   Electrical permit fee structure:
   - Base: $36.00
   - Solar system specific: ~$200-600 (varies by size)
   - Estimated total: $250-650
   - Verify current: (626) 458-3180
   
   Additional fees may apply:
   - Building permit (if required): $100-400
   - Utility application fee: $0-300 (varies by utility)

4. Wait for Approval
   Timeline:
   - LA County review: 2-4 weeks
   - Plan check required (professional engineer)
   - May require revisions: Add 1-2 weeks
   - Total: 3-6 weeks typical

PHASE 3: INSTALLATION (Licensed Solar Contractor)

Rooftop Solar Installation Steps:

1. Roof Preparation
   □ Verify roof structural integrity
   □ Mark rafter locations
   □ Plan penetration points (minimize holes)
   □ Install roof attachments (lag bolts into rafters)
   □ Flash penetrations (waterproofing critical!)

2. Mounting System Installation
   □ Install rail system on roof attachments
   □ Level and secure rails
   □ Verify proper spacing for panels
   □ Grounding connections to rails
   □ Proper drainage considerations

3. Solar Panel Installation
   □ Lift panels to roof (carefully!)
   □ Attach panels to rail system
   □ Connect panels in series/parallel per design
   □ Wire management (zip ties, cable clips)
   □ Verify all connections tight

4. Electrical Installation (Critical)
   
   DC Side (Solar panels to inverter):
   □ Run conduit from array to inverter location
   □ Install DC disconnect switch (roof or near inverter)
   □ Run appropriately sized wire (typically 10-12 AWG)
   □ Proper polarity (positive/negative clearly marked)
   □ Grounding electrode system for array
   
   Inverter Installation:
   □ Mount inverter (indoor or outdoor rated)
   □ Location: Near panel, accessible
   □ Proper clearances (6-36" depending on model)
   □ Connect DC input from array
   □ Connect AC output to panel
   
   AC Side (Inverter to electrical panel):
   □ Run conduit from inverter to main panel
   □ Install AC disconnect switch (required, near panel)
   □ Install appropriately sized breaker in panel
   □ Proper wire sizing (based on inverter output)
   □ GFCI not typically required for solar interconnection
   
   Grounding (Critical for safety):
   □ Ground solar array frame/rails
   □ Ground equipment (inverter, disconnects)
   □ Proper bonding of all metal components
   □ Grounding electrode conductor sized correctly
   □ Reference: 2022 CEC Article 690, Part VI

5. Labeling & Placarding (Required)
   Required labels:
   □ "Solar PV System" placard at main panel
   □ DC disconnect label with voltage/current ratings
   □ AC disconnect label with voltage/current ratings
   □ Inverter nameplate visible
   □ Roof access labels (for firefighters)
   □ Rapid shutdown labels (if applicable)
   □ Warning labels at all disconnects

6. Monitoring System Setup
   □ Install monitoring equipment (if included)
   □ Connect to WiFi/internet
   □ Verify production monitoring functioning
   □ Download app for homeowner

PHASE 4: INSPECTION (Multiple Inspections Required)

LA County Electrical Inspections:

Rough Inspection (if applicable):
- For underground conduit runs
- Before concrete/backfill
- Verify conduit routing, sizing

Final Electrical Inspection (Always required):
Inspector verifies:
□ Solar array properly installed and grounded
□ Conduit sizing adequate for wire fill
□ DC disconnect switch proper (rating, location, labeling)
□ AC disconnect switch proper (rating, location, labeling)
□ Inverter installed per manufacturer specs
□ Proper clearances around equipment
□ Wire sizing appropriate for current
□ Breaker sizing correct (120% rule followed)
□ All connections tight and secure
□ Proper grounding of all components
□ Grounding electrode system compliant
□ All required labeling present
□ System matches approved plans
□ No code violations

Common inspection points:
- Grounding: #1 inspection failure (improper bonding)
- Labeling: Must have all required placards
- Disconnects: Must be accessible and properly rated
- Wire sizing: Must match calculations
- Conduit fill: Cannot exceed 40% (typically)

If inspection PASSES:
✓ LA County approves system
✓ Receive inspection certificate
✓ Proceed to utility inspection

If inspection FAILS:
✗ Inspector notes violations
✗ Contractor fixes issues
✗ Request re-inspection through EPIC-LA
✗ Cannot proceed to utility inspection until LA County passes

Building Inspection (if building permit pulled):
- Separate from electrical inspection
- Verifies structural/roofing work
- Must also pass before utility inspection

PHASE 5: UTILITY INSPECTION & INTERCONNECTION

Utility Final Inspection:

1. Schedule Utility Inspection
   After LA County approval:
   □ Contact utility to schedule final inspection
   □ Provide LA County approval documentation
   □ Timeline: 1-4 weeks for utility inspection

2. Utility Inspector Verifies
   □ Meter socket/panel ready for bidirectional meter
   □ Disconnects accessible to utility
   □ System matches interconnection application
   □ Safety disconnect functions properly
   □ All utility requirements met

3. Utility Installs New Meter
   If utility inspection passes:
   □ Utility schedules meter installation (may be same visit)
   □ Install bidirectional meter (measures import/export)
   □ Activate net metering
   □ System officially energized!

4. Permission to Operate (PTO)
   □ Utility issues PTO letter/email
   □ Homeowner can turn on system
   □ Net metering begins
   □ Solar production starts!

PHASE 6: SYSTEM ACTIVATION & MONITORING

1. System Startup
   □ Contractor returns to activate system
   □ Verify inverter functioning
   □ Check production levels
   □ Verify monitoring system working

2. Homeowner Training
   □ Explain system operation
   □ Show monitoring app
   □ Explain disconnect switches
   □ Maintenance requirements
   □ Who to contact for issues

3. Ongoing Monitoring
   □ Check production daily (via app)
   □ Compare to expected output
   □ Watch for alerts/errors
   □ Contact installer if issues

INSPECTOR CHECKLIST: Solar PV Systems
================================================================================

WHAT LA COUNTY INSPECTOR WILL VERIFY:

SOLAR ARRAY:
□ Panels properly secured to mounting system
□ Mounting system attached to roof structure (rafters)
□ Roof penetrations properly flashed (waterproof)
□ Panel wiring properly secured and managed
□ Grounding of array frame/rails
□ Proper wire routing (protected from damage)

DC ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:
□ DC disconnect switch present and accessible
□ DC disconnect properly rated for system voltage/current
□ DC disconnect properly labeled ("Solar DC Disconnect")
□ DC wiring properly sized (based on short circuit current)
□ DC conduit properly installed and sized
□ Proper polarity maintained (positive/negative marked)
□ No damaged wire insulation

INVERTER:
□ Inverter installed per manufacturer specifications
□ Proper mounting (level, secure)
□ Adequate clearances maintained (ventilation)
□ Inverter properly grounded
□ DC input connected properly
□ AC output connected properly
□ Nameplate visible and legible

AC ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:
□ AC disconnect switch present and accessible
□ AC disconnect properly rated for system output
□ AC disconnect properly labeled ("Solar AC Disconnect")
□ AC wiring properly sized (based on inverter output)
□ AC conduit properly installed and sized
□ Breaker in main panel properly sized (120% rule followed)
□ Breaker located properly (not main breaker end if bus rating issue)
□ Proper connection to main panel

GROUNDING & BONDING:
□ Solar array frame properly grounded
□ Mounting rails bonded together
□ Grounding electrode conductor properly sized
□ Grounding electrode system compliant (ground rods, etc.)
□ All equipment grounding conductors present
□ Inverter grounding proper
□ All metal enclosures bonded
□ Proper torque on grounding connections

LABELING & PLACARDING:
□ Main panel placard: "Solar PV System" with specs
□ DC disconnect label with voltage/current ratings
□ AC disconnect label with voltage/current ratings
□ Inverter nameplate visible
□ Rapid shutdown labeling (if system requires it)
□ Roof access placards (for firefighters)
□ Warning labels at all disconnect points
□ Wire identification labels (DC+, DC-, AC)

CALCULATIONS & COMPLIANCE:
□ System matches approved plans
□ Wire sizing calculations correct
□ Conduit fill calculations correct (max 40%)
□ Breaker sizing follows 120% rule
□ Short circuit current calculations provided
□ Grounding electrode conductor sizing correct

SAFETY SYSTEMS:
□ Rapid shutdown system (if required by code year)
□ Arc fault protection (inverter feature)
□ Ground fault protection (inverter feature)
□ All disconnects accessible and functioning
□ No energized exposed conductors

COMMON INSPECTOR FINDINGS (Violations):

❌ VIOLATION 1: Inadequate Grounding
   Problem: Array frame not properly grounded to earth
   Why it fails: Lightning protection, safety requirement
   Fix: Install proper grounding electrode system, bonding
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.43-690.47
   Cost to fix: $200-500

❌ VIOLATION 2: Missing or Incorrect Labels
   Problem: Required placards/labels not present or incorrect
   Why it fails: Code requires specific labeling for safety
   Fix: Install all required labels with correct information
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.56
   Cost to fix: $50-150 (labels + labor)

❌ VIOLATION 3: DC Disconnect Not Accessible
   Problem: DC disconnect switch blocked or too high
   Why it fails: Must be accessible for safety
   Fix: Relocate disconnect or remove obstruction
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.15
   Cost to fix: $100-400 (depends on relocation)

❌ VIOLATION 4: Improper Wire Sizing
   Problem: DC or AC wiring undersized for current
   Why it fails: Fire hazard, voltage drop issues
   Fix: Replace with properly sized conductors
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.8, 310.12
   Cost to fix: $300-1,000 (rewiring)

❌ VIOLATION 5: 120% Rule Violation
   Problem: Main breaker + solar breaker exceeds 120% of bus bar rating
   Why it fails: Panel overload risk
   Fix: Service upgrade OR reduce system size OR use line-side tap
   Reference: 2022 CEC 705.12(B)(2)
   Cost to fix: $2,000-5,000 (service upgrade)

❌ VIOLATION 6: Conduit Fill Exceeded
   Problem: Too many wires in conduit (over 40%)
   Why it fails: Heat buildup, wire damage risk
   Fix: Install larger conduit or reduce wire count
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.31, Table 1 (conduit fill)
   Cost to fix: $200-600 (new conduit)

❌ VIOLATION 7: Missing AC Disconnect
   Problem: No AC disconnect switch between inverter and panel
   Why it fails: Required for safety (isolation)
   Fix: Install AC disconnect switch
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.15
   Cost to fix: $150-400

❌ VIOLATION 8: Improper Roof Flashing
   Problem: Roof penetrations not properly waterproofed
   Why it fails: Water intrusion risk (not code but critical)
   Fix: Install proper flashing at all penetrations
   Reference: Building code (not electrical)
   Cost to fix: $500-1,500

❌ VIOLATION 9: Inverter Clearance Inadequate
   Problem: Inverter installed too close to combustibles or without ventilation
   Why it fails: Fire risk, overheating
   Fix: Relocate inverter or remove obstructions
   Reference: 2022 CEC 110.26, manufacturer specs
   Cost to fix: $300-800 (relocation)

❌ VIOLATION 10: Grounding Electrode Conductor Wrong Size
   Problem: Ground wire to ground rods undersized
   Why it fails: Inadequate fault protection
   Fix: Replace with properly sized conductor
   Reference: 2022 CEC 250.166, Table 250.66
   Cost to fix: $150-400

PERMIT REQUIREMENTS: Solar PV Systems
================================================================================

PERMIT DECISION TREE:

Solar PV installation:
  │
  ├─ Any grid-tied solar system?
  │  └─ YES → ELECTRICAL PERMIT REQUIRED (always)
  │     Fee: $36 base + solar fees (~$250-650 total)
  │     Process: EPIC-LA (epicla.lacounty.gov)
  │     Applicant: Licensed C-10 or C-46 contractor
  │     Plans: Professional engineer stamped plans required
  │     Utility approval: Must obtain before installation
  │
  ├─ Building modifications (roof, structure)?
  │  └─ YES → BUILDING PERMIT also required
  │     Fee: Additional $100-400
  │     Process: LA County Building & Safety
  │
  ├─ Ground-mounted system?
  │  └─ YES → ELECTRICAL + BUILDING PERMITS required
  │     Zoning: Check setback requirements
  │     Fee: $300-800 total
  │
  └─ Battery storage added?
     └─ YES → Noted on electrical permit
        Additional fees: May apply
        Inspection: Battery system inspected separately

PERMIT APPLICATION PROCESS:

Step 1: Obtain Utility Approval FIRST
  Critical: Must have utility interconnection approval before LA County permit
  
  Process:
  1. Contact utility (SCE, LADWP, etc.)
  2. Submit interconnection application
  3. Provide system design details
  4. Wait for utility approval (2-8 weeks)
  5. Receive approval letter
  
  Without utility approval:
  → LA County will NOT issue permit
  → This is the most common delay

Step 2: Prepare Required Documents
  Professional documents needed:
  □ Site plan showing roof layout and panel locations
  □ Single-line electrical diagram
  □ Three-line electrical diagram (if required)
  □ Equipment specifications (panels, inverter, disconnects)
  □ Wire sizing calculations
  □ Conduit fill calculations
  □ Load calculations (120% rule verification)
  □ Short circuit current calculations
  □ Grounding calculations
  □ Structural calculations (roof load analysis)
  □ Professional engineer stamp (usually required)
  □ Utility interconnection approval letter
  
  Note: Most solar contractors prepare these documents

Step 3: Apply via EPIC-LA
  URL: epicla.lacounty.gov
  Permit type: "Solar Photovoltaic System - Electrical"
  
  Applicant requirements:
  - Licensed C-10 (General Electrical Contractor) OR
  - Licensed C-46 (Solar Contractor)
  - Both licenses allow solar electrical work
  - Must be California licensed and insured

Step 4: Upload All Documents
  In EPIC-LA system:
  □ Upload all engineering documents (PDFs)
  □ Upload utility approval letter
  □ Equipment cut sheets (panels, inverter specs)
  □ Roof structural evaluation (if required)
  □ Photos of installation site (helpful)

Step 5: Pay Permit Fees
  Fee breakdown:
  - Base electrical permit: $36.00 (2023)
  - Solar system fee: ~$200-600 (varies by system size)
  - Total estimated: $250-650
  - Exact fees: Verify with LA County (626) 458-3180
  
  Payment in EPIC-LA:
  - Credit/debit card accepted
  - Fees paid before plan check begins

Step 6: Plan Check Review
  LA County reviews:

COMMON VIOLATIONS: Solar PV Systems
================================================================================

TOP 10 SOLAR PV VIOLATIONS:

VIOLATION 1: Inadequate Grounding System
❌ Problem: Array not properly grounded to earth
   Why it fails: Lightning protection, safety requirement
   How to fix: Install proper grounding electrode system
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.43-690.47
   Cost to fix: $200-500

VIOLATION 2: Missing Required Labels
❌ Problem: Placards/labels not present or incorrect
   Why it fails: Code requires specific safety labeling
   How to fix: Install all required labels with correct specs
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.56
   Cost to fix: $50-150

VIOLATION 3: DC Disconnect Inaccessible
❌ Problem: DC disconnect blocked or too high
   Why it fails: Must be accessible for safety
   How to fix: Relocate disconnect or remove obstruction
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.15
   Cost to fix: $100-400

VIOLATION 4: Wire Sizing Incorrect
❌ Problem: DC or AC wiring undersized
   Why it fails: Fire hazard, voltage drop
   How to fix: Replace with proper sized conductors
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.8, 310.12
   Cost to fix: $300-1,000

VIOLATION 5: 120% Rule Violated
❌ Problem: Main + solar breakers exceed 120% of bus bar
   Why it fails: Panel overload risk
   How to fix: Service upgrade OR reduce system OR line-side tap
   Reference: 2022 CEC 705.12(B)(2)
   Cost to fix: $2,000-5,000 (if service upgrade needed)

VIOLATION 6: Conduit Overfilled
❌ Problem: Too many wires in conduit
   Why it fails: Heat buildup, wire damage
   How to fix: Larger conduit or reduce wires
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.31, Table 1
   Cost to fix: $200-600

VIOLATION 7: Missing AC Disconnect
❌ Problem: No AC disconnect between inverter and panel
   Why it fails: Required for safety isolation
   How to fix: Install AC disconnect switch
   Reference: 2022 CEC 690.15
   Cost to fix: $150-400

VIOLATION 8: Improper Roof Flashing
❌ Problem: Roof penetrations not waterproofed
   Why it fails: Water intrusion risk
   How to fix: Install proper flashing
   Reference: Building code
   Cost to fix: $500-1,500

VIOLATION 9: Inverter Clearance Issues
❌ Problem: Inverter too close to combustibles
   Why it fails: Fire risk, overheating
   How to fix: Relocate inverter
   Reference: 2022 CEC 110.26
   Cost to fix: $300-800

VIOLATION 10: Ground Wire Wrong Size
❌ Problem: Grounding electrode conductor undersized
   Why it fails: Inadequate fault protection
   How to fix: Replace with correct size
   Reference: 2022 CEC 250.166, Table 250.66
   Cost to fix: $150-400

RAG QUERY EXAMPLES

Q: "How much does solar cost in LA County?"
A: "Typical residential solar costs: 4 kW ($11,050-20,400), 7 kW ($17,900-31,200), 
10 kW ($24,800-42,750) before incentives. After 30% federal tax credit: 
$7,735-29,925. Cost: $2.40-4.00 per watt installed. Includes permits ($250-650), 
equipment, installation. Battery backup adds $9,000-17,000. Get quotes from licensed 
C-10 or C-46 contractors."

Q: "Do I need permit for solar panels?"
A: "YES. All grid-tied solar requires electrical permit from LA County. Fee: 
$250-650 (2023). Apply via EPIC-LA (epicla.lacounty.gov). Must have utility 
interconnection approval FIRST. Licensed C-10 or C-46 contractor pulls permit. 
Professional engineer stamped plans required. Timeline: 2-4 weeks approval + 
3-6 weeks installation + inspections."

Q: "Can I install solar myself?"
A: "Not recommended and usually not permitted. Solar requires: (1) Licensed C-10 or 
C-46 contractor, (2) Professional engineer plans, (3) Utility approval, 
(4) LA County permit, (5) Multiple inspections. DIY solar typically not allowed for 
grid-tied systems. Hire licensed contractor."

Q: "Do I need to upgrade my electrical service for solar?"
A: "Maybe. Use 120% rule: (Main breaker) + (Solar breaker) ≤ 120% of bus bar rating. 
Example: 200A service + 40A solar = 240A. If 240A ≤ (200A × 1.2 = 240A) → OK. 
If exceeds 120%: Service upgrade needed ($2,000-5,000). Most 200A services handle 
6-10 kW solar without upgrade. 100A services often need upgrade."

Q: "What does inspector check for solar?"
A: "Inspector verifies: (1) Array properly grounded, (2) All required labels present, 
(3) DC/AC disconnects accessible and rated correctly, (4) Wire sizing appropriate, 
(5) 120% rule followed for breaker sizing, (6) Conduit fill correct, (7) Inverter 
clearances adequate, (8) System matches approved plans, (9) All equipment bonded, 
(10) Roof penetrations flashed. See cross-reference-solar-pv-LA.txt for complete 
checklist."

Q: "How long does solar permit take?"
A: "Timeline: Utility approval (2-8 weeks) → LA County permit application → Plan 
check (2-4 weeks) → Permit issued → Installation (3-5 days) → LA County inspection 
(schedule 1-3 weeks ahead) → Utility final inspection (1-4 weeks) → PTO. 
Total: 2-4 months from start to energized system. Delays common if corrections needed."

Q: "What's the 120% rule for solar?"
A: "Formula: (Main breaker rating) + (Solar breaker rating) ≤ 120% of panel bus bar 
rating. Prevents panel overload. Example: 200A panel, 40A solar breaker. Check: 
200A + 40A = 240A. Panel bus bar × 1.2 = 200A × 1.2 = 240A. Result: 240A ≤ 240A → OK! 
If exceeds: Need service upgrade OR reduce solar size OR line-side tap. Reference: 
2022 CEC 705.12(B)(2)."

Q: "Do I get tax credit for solar in California?"
A: "YES. Federal tax credit: 30% of solar system cost (2023-2032). California state 
incentives: SGIP for battery storage (up to $4,000). Utility rebates: Check with 
SCE/LADWP. Example: $20,000 solar system. Federal tax credit: $6,000 (claim on taxes). 
Net cost: $14,000. Service upgrades NOT eligible for tax credit."
