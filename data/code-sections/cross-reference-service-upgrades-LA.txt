🚨 SERVICE UPGRADE ALERTS - CRITICAL REQUIREMENTS:

⚠️ C-10 LICENSE MANDATORY:
   Only C-10 (electrical contractor) licensed professionals can:
   • Pull service upgrade permits
   • Coordinate with utilities
   • Perform service entrance work
   • Sign off on installations
   CONSEQUENCE: Without license = cannot legally perform work

⚠️ UTILITY COORDINATION MANDATORY:
   Before any work begins:
   • Contact utility (SCE or LADWP for LA County)
   • Utility must disconnect power
   • Utility may provide new meter
   • Utility coordinates reconnection after inspection
   TIMELINE: 1-2 weeks to schedule disconnection

⚠️ POWER OUTAGE REQUIRED:
   During installation:
   • House loses ALL power (1-3 days typical)
   • No refrigeration, heating/cooling, water pump
   • Customer must plan accordingly
   • Plan for elderly/medical device customers carefully

⚠️ TWO GROUND RODS MINIMUM:
   Most common violation:
   • Code requires TWO ground rods (minimum)
   • Spaced 6 feet apart (minimum)
   • Both bonded to service ground
   • Inspector will fail if only one rod present

⚠️ NEUTRAL-GROUND BOND AT SERVICE ONLY:
   Critical bonding rule:
   • Bond neutral to ground at SERVICE ENTRANCE ONLY
   • NEVER bond at sub-panels (creates hazard)
   • Most common violation finding

⚠️ WIRE SIZING CRITICAL FOR SAFETY:
   200A service example:
   • Must use 2/0 AWG copper OR 4/0 AWG aluminum
   • Undersized wire = FIRE HAZARD
   • Inspector will fail if wire undersized
   • Common cost-cutting mistake

DECISION TREE: SERVICE UPGRADE vs. PANEL-ONLY vs. SUB-PANEL
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WORK REQUIRED ANALYSIS:

Option 1: FULL SERVICE UPGRADE (Most Common)

When needed:
→ Current service inadequate for total load
→ Upgrading old home (60A or 100A to 200A)
→ Adding major equipment (EV + pool + HVAC)
→ Frequent breaker trips (capacity exceeded)

What's included:
✓ New meter socket (larger amperage rating)
✓ New service entrance cable/conduit
✓ New main breaker (200A typical)
✓ New panel enclosure (larger)
✓ New grounding system (2 ground rods minimum)
✓ Utility coordination for meter upgrade

Cost: $2,450-5,400 (typical $3,000-4,000)
Permit: Required ($150-400 fee)
Permit type: "Service Upgrade"
Timeline: 4-6 weeks
Power outage: 1-3 days
Best for: Homes needing more capacity

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Option 2: PANEL REPLACEMENT ONLY

When needed:
→ Current service adequate (has capacity)
→ Panel damaged/outdated but service sufficient
→ No more breaker slots available
→ Upgrading from fuses to breakers

What's included:
✓ New panel enclosure (same or larger amp rating)
✓ New breakers (transfer from old or new)
✓ Same service entrance (no utility work)
✓ Same meter (no upgrade)
✓ Existing grounding system (usually adequate)

Cost: $1,900-3,800 (typical $2,500-3,000)
Permit: Required ($100-250 fee)
Permit type: "Electrical Panel Replacement"
Timeline: 2-3 weeks
Power outage: 4-8 hours (may be brief)
Best for: Homes with adequate service, old panel

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Option 3: SUB-PANEL ADDITION

When needed:
→ Main service has capacity (not fully loaded)
→ Need more breaker slots (main panel full)
→ Remote location needs circuits (garage, ADU)
→ Want to keep existing main panel

What's included:
✓ New sub-panel enclosure
✓ Feeder circuit from main panel to sub-panel
✓ New breakers (only in sub-panel)
✓ Grounding (to main service ground, NOT separate rods)
✓ NO changes to main service entrance

Cost: $1,300-2,750 (typical $1,800-2,200)
Permit: Required ($100-200 fee)
Permit type: "Sub-Panel Installation" or "Feeder Circuit"
Timeline: 1-2 weeks
Power outage: Minimal (usually <1 hour)
Best for: Adding capacity without upgrading service

COMPLETE STEP-BY-STEP: SERVICE UPGRADE PROCESS (100A→200A EXAMPLE)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PHASE 1: ASSESSMENT & PLANNING (Week 1)

Step 1: Determine Current Service
□ Find main breaker in panel
□ Note amp rating (60A, 100A, 150A, etc.)
□ Check meter for service size
□ If unsure: Call licensed electrician ($75-150 assessment)

Step 2: Calculate Load Requirements
Use formula:
  3,000W (base) + 3,000W (kitchen/laundry) + Fixed appliances + HVAC + New loads

Example:
  3,000 + 3,000 + 5,000 (range, water heater, dryer) + 7,200 (AC) + 9,600 (EV) = 30,800W
  30,800W ÷ 240V = 128 amps
  → Need 200A service (128A < 160A continuous @ 200A available)

Step 3: Get Quotes from 3 Contractors
Requirements:
□ C-10 electrical contractor license
□ Current liability insurance ($1M+ recommended)
□ References from previous service upgrades
□ Written quote with:
  - Scope of work
  - Timeline
  - Permit cost
  - Materials itemized
  - Labor hours/rate
  - Total price
  - Payment terms

Step 4: Select Contractor & Sign Agreement
□ Review all three quotes
□ Select contractor (not always lowest price)
□ Consider experience, timeline, references
□ Sign contract with terms specified
□ Get copy for records

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PHASE 2: PERMIT APPLICATION (Week 1-2)

Step 1: Contractor Prepares Permit Application
Documentation needed:
□ Current service rating (100A)
□ Proposed service rating (200A)
□ Panel location plan
□ Load calculation worksheet
□ Single-line electrical diagram (simple drawing)
□ Contractor license number
□ Contractor contact info

Step 2: Submit via EPIC-LA
URL: epicla.lacounty.gov
Process:
□ Create account (if new contractor)
□ Select: "New Permit"
□ Category: "Electrical" → "Service Upgrade"
□ Enter all required information
□ Upload load calculation + diagram
□ Enter payment method (credit card)
□ Submit application
□ Note permit number from confirmation

Step 3: LA County Reviews Application
Timeline:
□ Simple 200A upgrade: 5-7 days
□ Complex/special cases: 10-15 days
□ If corrections needed: 5-7 days additional

LA County may request:
□ Additional load calculations
□ Clarification on grounding method
□ Professional engineer stamp (rare for residential)

Step 4: Permit Approved
□ Receive approval email from EPIC-LA
□ Print approval document
□ Approval includes: Permit number, conditions, inspection requirements
□ Ready to proceed with utility coordination

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PHASE 3: UTILITY COORDINATION (Week 2-4)

Step 1: Contact Utility Company
For unincorporated LA County:
□ Identify which utility (SCE or LADWP)
□ Call to request: "Service upgrade disconnect/reconnection"
□ Provide: Address, current service amperage, proposed amperage

Utility contacts:
□ Southern California Edison: 1-800-655-4555
□ LA Dept Water & Power: 1-800-342-5397

Step 2: Schedule Disconnection
Utility will:
□ Verify service location
□ Check for hazards
□ Schedule disconnect appointment (1-2 weeks out)
□ Provide disconnect date/time window
□ Notify contractor of timing
□ May require signature at disconnection

Step 3: Coordinate with Contractor
Before disconnection day:
□ Verify contractor availability (same day as disconnect)
□ Confirm contractor has all materials on-site
□ Plan timing (morning disconnect = full day work)
□ Communicate with customer (house without power)
□ Arrange for emergency services if needed (elderly, medical)

Step 4: Pre-Work Inspection (Optional but Recommended)
Utility may do:
□ Visual inspection of service area
□ Check for safety hazards
□ Verify contractor's plan
□ Note any special requirements

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PHASE 4: INSTALLATION (Week 4-5) - Power Off

Disconnection Day Timeline:

Morning (Before 8 AM):
□ Utility arrives to disconnect power at meter
□ Contractor on-site before utility
□ Customer prepared (no refrigeration access)
□ Contractor confirms permit/plans with utility
□ Utility cuts power at meter line

Installation Work (Days 1-2):

SERVICE ENTRANCE WORK:
□ Remove old meter socket (utility may do this)
□ Install new 200A meter socket
□ Run new service entrance cable:
  - 2/0 AWG copper (or 4/0 aluminum)
  - In conduit for protection
  - Proper weatherhead at top (if overhead)
  - Grounding lug at meter
□ Connect grounding/bonding cables

GROUNDING SYSTEM (Critical):
□ Locate first ground rod location (near meter/panel)
□ Drive copper ground rod 8+ feet into soil:
  - 5/8" diameter rod minimum
  - 8 feet minimum length
  - In soil (NOT concrete)
□ Locate second ground rod location (6+ feet from first):
  - Same specifications as first rod
  - 6+ feet separation minimum
□ Bond both rods together:
  - #4 AWG copper conductor minimum (for 200A service)
  - Pressure connectors or solder
  - Both rods must test <25Ω to earth
□ Connect service ground electrode conductor:
  - #4 AWG copper from main panel to bonded rods

MAIN PANEL INSTALLATION:
□ Remove old 100A panel from wall
□ Install new 200A panel in same/similar location
□ Verify clearances met:
  - 36" width minimum
  - 30" depth minimum
  - 6.5' height minimum
  - No obstructions in front
□ Secure panel to wall (proper fasteners)
□ Install main breaker (200A) in top position
□ Transfer existing circuits to new panel:
  - Map old breaker→wire→locations
  - Install new breakers (same amp as old, unless recalculating)
  - Connect wires to new breaker terminals
  - Keep same arrangement (similar to old for safety)
  - Install AFCI/GFCI breakers where required

BONDING & LABELING:
□ Install main bonding jumper at new panel:
  - Connects neutral to ground (at service only)
  - #8 AWG copper or larger
  - Securely attached
□ Label main breaker: "200 Amp Service"
□ Create circuit directory showing:
  - Breaker number
  - Circuit description (bedroom, kitchen, etc.)
  - Breaker amperage
□ Install "Danger High Voltage" labels if applicable
□ Label service entrance amperage/voltage

Work Progress Check:
□ Day 1: Service entrance + meter + grounding complete
□ Day 2: Main panel installed + circuits transferred + labeling done
□ Ready for inspection

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PHASE 5: REQUEST & PASS INSPECTION (Week 5-7)

Step 1: Request Final Inspection via EPIC-LA
□ Log into epicla.lacounty.gov
□ Find your permit
□ Select: "Request Inspection"
□ Choose: "Final Inspection" (or "Rough" if applicable)
□ Select appointment date/time window
□ Confirm contractor will be present
□ Receive confirmation

Step 2: Prepare for Inspection
Contractor should verify:
□ All work complete per code
□ Circuit directory accurate and legible
□ Main breaker functioning properly
□ Clearances maintained (36" width, etc.)
□ Ground rods properly installed (may need to show rods)
□ Grounding continuity verified with meter
□ All breakers properly sized
□ No double-tapped breakers
□ AFCI/GFCI breakers installed where required
□ Bonding connections tight

Step 3: Inspector Arrives (Final Inspection)
Inspector verification (30-60 minutes):

Visual inspection:
□ Panel location and clearances
□ Service entrance cable sizing
□ Meter socket installation
□ Ground rods visible (may ask to see)
□ Main breaker size and operation
□ Circuit labeling completeness

Testing performed:
□ Continuity/resistance checks (if equipped)
□ Ground resistance measurement (should be <25Ω)
□ Bonding continuity verification
□ Breaker operation (on/off cycles)
□ Main breaker functionality

Documentation:
□ Verifies load calculations
□ Reviews bonding sketch
□ Checks grounding system details
□ Signs off if all compliant

Step 4: Inspection Result

IF PASSES ✓:
□ Inspector approves work
□ Receives "Approval" notation in system
□ LA County notifies utility
□ Ready for reconnection

IF FAILS ✗:
□ Inspector provides violation list
□ Common failures:
  - Undersized wire (need 2/0 copper for 200A)
  - Only one ground rod (need two)
  - Ground resistance >25Ω
  - Panel not properly secured
  - AFCI/GFCI missing
  - Double-tapped breakers found
  - Clearances inadequate
□ Contractor corrects violations
□ Request re-inspection
□ Wait another 1-3 weeks

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PHASE 6: UTILITY RECONNECTION (Week 7-8)

After inspection PASSES:

Step 1: Notify Utility of Approval
□ LA County may automatically notify utility
□ OR contractor calls utility directly
□ Provide: Permit number, inspection approval date

Step 2: Schedule Reconnection
Utility will:
□ Schedule reconnection appointment (typically 24-48 hours after approval)
□ May install new meter
□ Reconnect power at meter
□ Test service for proper function

Step 3: Contractor Verification
□ Contractor present during reconnection (optional)
□ Verifies all breakers function correctly
□ Tests for proper voltage (240V across service)
□ Confirms all circuits energized
□ Checks for any unusual sounds/smells

Step 4: Customer Sign-Off
□ Customer accepts work
□ Contractor provides final documentation:
  - Permit approval document
  - Circuit directory
  - Grounding details (for records)
  - Warranty information
□ Keep records for future reference

Project COMPLETE ✓

SERVICE UPGRADE - PRE-INSPECTION CHECKLIST (Ready to Use)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🛠️ COMPLETE PRE-INSPECTION PROCEDURE - Day Before Final Inspection:

CHECKLIST SECTION 1: SERVICE ENTRANCE VERIFICATION
□ Verify wire size is correct (2/0 AWG copper or 4/0 aluminum for 200A)
□ Check wire is in protective conduit (not exposed)
□ Inspect weatherhead (if overhead service) - properly installed
□ Verify no damage to wire or insulation
□ Check service entrance attachment point (secure, proper height)
□ Verify grounding/bonding wires are present
□ Document: "Service entrance verified correct size and installation"

CHECKLIST SECTION 2: METER SOCKET VERIFICATION
□ Meter socket installed: Correct amperage (200A for 200A service)
□ Socket securely mounted to house
□ No damage to socket
□ Grounding lug present at meter
□ Verify utility hasn't yet installed meter (if pending)
□ Document: "Meter socket installed correctly"

CHECKLIST SECTION 3: GROUNDING SYSTEM VERIFICATION (Critical)
□ Locate first ground rod (should be visible in soil)
□ Verify in SOIL (NOT concrete or asphalt)
□ Measure spacing to second ground rod: 6+ feet minimum
□ Locate second ground rod in soil, same specifications
□ Check bonding between rods:
  □ Copper conductor connecting both rods
  □ #4 AWG or larger (for 200A)
  □ Continuity with ohmmeter: should read <1 ohm
□ Check ground electrode conductor:
  □ Connects bonded rods to service panel
  □ #4 AWG copper minimum
  □ Properly secured along route
  □ No damage
□ Test total ground resistance (if meter available):
  □ Should read <25 ohms to earth
   (If >25 ohms: May fail inspection, need additional rod)
□ Document: "Ground rods verified 6+ feet apart, <1 ohm bonding, <25 ohm resistance"

CHECKLIST SECTION 4: MAIN PANEL VERIFICATION
□ Panel is 200A rated (check label/nameplate)
□ Main breaker is 200A (correct size)
□ Panel secured to wall (no movement when pressed)
□ Clearances adequate:
  □ 36" width minimum (measure from panel edges)
  □ 30" depth minimum (in front of panel)
  □ 6.5' height minimum (from ground to top)
□ No obstructions blocking access
□ Panel location is accessible (not in bedroom, bathroom, closet)
□ Document: "Panel installation verified, clearances adequate"

CHECKLIST SECTION 5: BONDING VERIFICATION (Critical)
□ Main bonding jumper installed at service:
  □ Connects neutral to ground bus (at service entrance ONLY)
  □ #8 AWG copper or larger
  □ Securely attached with appropriate clamps
□ Verify NO bonding at sub-panels (if any present):
  □ Sub-panels should NOT bond neutral to ground
  □ This creates safety hazard
□ Test continuity of bonding:
  □ Ohmmeter between neutral and ground: <1 ohm
□ Document: "Main bonding jumper verified at service entrance only"

CHECKLIST SECTION 6: CIRCUIT BREAKER VERIFICATION
□ Main breaker: 200A rated, ON position, functions smoothly
□ Test breaker ON/OFF function multiple times (should operate smoothly)
□ Branch breakers: All properly installed in slots
□ No double-tapped breakers (two wires on one terminal):
  □ Exception: Breaker explicitly rated for two conductors
  □ Otherwise: One wire per breaker terminal only
□ AFCI breakers installed on:
  □ Bedroom circuits (all require AFCI per 2022 CEC)
  □ Verify "AFCI" label on breakers
□ GFCI breakers installed on:
  □ Bathroom circuits
  □ Kitchen counter circuits
  □ Garage circuits
  □ Outdoor circuits
  □ Verify "GFCI" label on breakers
□ Breaker amperage matches circuit wire gauge:
  □ 15A breaker: #14 AWG wire minimum
  □ 20A breaker: #12 AWG wire minimum
  □ 30A breaker: #10 AWG wire minimum
  □ 40A+ breaker: Proper wire size per amp rating
□ Document: "All breakers properly sized and installed, AFCI/GFCI present"

CHECKLIST SECTION 7: CIRCUIT DIRECTORY VERIFICATION
□ Directory label securely attached inside panel door
□ All circuits listed (no blanks)
□ Each circuit has description:
  □ Room/area (e.g., "Master Bedroom", "Kitchen")
  □ Breaker amperage (e.g., "20A", "30A")
□ Descriptions are legible and permanent (not pencil if permanent)
□ Directory matches actual wiring:
  □ Check 3-5 random circuits
  □ Verify breaker number matches description
  □ Verify breaker amperage matches
□ Document: "Circuit directory complete and accurate"

CHECKLIST SECTION 8: LABELING VERIFICATION
□ Main panel labeled: "200 Amp Service" (or appropriate size)
□ Meter location labeled (if applicable)
□ "Danger High Voltage" labels present (if applicable)
□ Grounding system labeled (if visible)
□ All labels permanent (not pencil for permanent work)
□ Document: "All required labeling installed"

CHECKLIST SECTION 9: FINAL SAFETY CHECK
□ No exposed live parts
□ No water infiltration or moisture
□ No damaged equipment
□ No improvised connections or repairs
□ Everything appears professional and complete
□ Document: "Final safety verification complete"

SERVICE UPGRADE - COMMON FAILURES & PREVENTION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

TOP 10 SERVICE UPGRADE INSPECTION FAILURES:

❌ FAILURE 1: "Undersized Service Entrance Conductor"
CAUSE: Using #1 or #2 AWG on 200A service (cost cutting)
CONSEQUENCE: Wire can't handle 200A safely = fire hazard
PREVENTION: Use 2/0 AWG copper or 4/0 aluminum minimum
FIX: Replace entire service entrance wire (expensive)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $400-800 to fix

❌ FAILURE 2: "Only One Ground Rod (Need Two)"
CAUSE: Single ground rod installation (common mistake)
CONSEQUENCE: Inadequate grounding = safety hazard
PREVENTION: Always install two rods, 6+ feet apart
FIX: Install second ground rod + bond to first
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $150-300 to fix

❌ FAILURE 3: "Ground Rods in Concrete (Not Soil)"
CAUSE: Concrete equipment pad mistaken for soil
CONSEQUENCE: Concrete non-conductive = ineffective grounding
PREVENTION: Install rods in SOIL only (test with shovel)
FIX: Relocate rods to soil area
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $200-400 to fix

❌ FAILURE 4: "Ground Resistance >25 Ohms"
CAUSE: Poor soil contact or inadequate rod depth
CONSEQUENCE: Fault protection compromised
PREVENTION: Install rods 8+ feet deep, test resistance
FIX: Add additional ground rod or improve soil contact
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $100-300 to fix

❌ FAILURE 5: "Neutral-Ground Bond at Sub-Panel"
CAUSE: Bonding at both service and sub-panel
CONSEQUENCE: Creates parallel neutral path = hazard
PREVENTION: Bond ONLY at service entrance, never sub-panels
FIX: Remove improper bonding from sub-panel
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks (quick fix)
COST: $50-150 to fix

❌ FAILURE 6: "Inadequate Working Clearance"
CAUSE: Panel too close to door, obstruction present
CONSEQUENCE: Unsafe access for maintenance
PREVENTION: Verify 36" width, 30" depth clearance
FIX: Relocate panel (major expense if required)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $1,000-3,000+ to fix (if relocation needed)

❌ FAILURE 7: "Missing AFCI Breakers"
CAUSE: Bedroom circuits installed with standard breakers
CONSEQUENCE: No arc-fault protection per code
PREVENTION: Install AFCI breakers on ALL bedroom circuits
FIX: Replace breakers with AFCI-rated versions
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $40-80 per breaker to fix

❌ FAILURE 8: "Double-Tapped Breakers"
CAUSE: Two wires on one breaker terminal (not rated for it)
CONSEQUENCE: Loose connection = fire hazard
PREVENTION: One wire per breaker terminal (unless rated)
FIX: Install separate breaker for second circuit
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $50-150 to fix

❌ FAILURE 9: "Incorrect Circuit Directory"
CAUSE: Directory incomplete or inaccurate
CONSEQUENCE: Safety hazard (can't identify circuits)
PREVENTION: Verify directory matches all breakers before inspection
FIX: Update directory with correct information
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: Often waived for minor fixes
COST: $0-50 (usually quick fix)

❌ FAILURE 10: "Wrong Main Breaker Amperage"
CAUSE: 100A breaker in 200A panel (or vice versa)
CONSEQUENCE: Doesn't match service rating = safety issue
PREVENTION: Main breaker must match service amperage exactly
FIX: Replace main breaker with correct amperage
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-3 weeks
COST: $30-80 to fix

PREVENTION STRATEGY:
→ Use 9-section pre-inspection checklist (above) DAY BEFORE inspection
→ Verify wire size, ground rods, bonding, clearances yourself
→ Fix any problems BEFORE inspector arrives
→ Result: PASS first inspection, no re-inspection delays

SERVICE UPGRADE - MYTHS vs REALITY
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

❌ MYTH 1: "One ground rod is sufficient (save cost)"
✅ REALITY: Code REQUIRES two ground rods minimum (2022 CEC 250.53)
   REASON: Single rod ineffective for fault protection
   CONSEQUENCE: Inspector will fail, must install two
   COST: Small increase ($100-150 extra) worth the safety
   VERDICT: Two rods non-negotiable requirement

❌ MYTH 2: "I can bond ground rod in concrete pad (easier)"
✅ REALITY: Ground rod MUST be in SOIL (not concrete)
   REASON: Concrete non-conductive (electricity can't reach earth)
   CONSEQUENCE: Inspector will fail, major expense to fix
   FIX COST: $200-400 to relocate
   VERDICT: Install in soil from start to avoid re-work

❌ MYTH 3: "Undersized wire saves money (temporary solution)"
✅ REALITY: Undersized wire creates FIRE HAZARD
   FACT: #2 AWG on 200A = Overheating, melting insulation
   CONSEQUENCE: Fire risk + inspector failure + hazard
   FIX COST: $400-800 to replace entire service wire
   VERDICT: Proper wire sizing is non-negotiable for safety

❌ MYTH 4: "Panel can go anywhere (closet, bathroom, garage wall)"
✅ REALITY: Code restricts panel locations
   PROHIBITED: Bathrooms, clothes closets, tight spaces
   REQUIRED: Accessible location with adequate clearance
   CONSEQUENCE: Inspector will fail, may require relocation
   FIX COST: $1,000-3,000+ if relocation needed
   VERDICT: Plan panel location carefully before installation

❌ MYTH 5: "Bonding neutral at sub-panel provides extra protection"
✅ REALITY: Sub-panel bonding creates SAFETY HAZARD
   REASON: Creates parallel neutral path (shock risk)
   CONSEQUENCE: Inspector will fail, must remove bonding
   PROPER: Bond neutral-to-ground at SERVICE ONLY
   VERDICT: Sub-panels must NOT have neutral-ground bonding

❌ MYTH 6: "DIY service upgrade possible to save money"
✅ REALITY: CANNOT do service upgrade without C-10 license
   REASON: Must coordinate with utility, complex work
   CONSEQUENCE: Permit denied, insurance void, safety hazard
   REQUIREMENT: Licensed C-10 electrical contractor only
   VERDICT: Service upgrade is NOT DIY work

❌ MYTH 7: "Service upgrade takes 1-2 days"
✅ REALITY: Service upgrade typically 4-6 weeks
   BREAKDOWN: Permit (5-7 days) + Utility (1-2 weeks) + Work (1-3 days) + 
              Inspection (1-3 weeks) + Reconnection (1-2 days)
   CRITICAL: Coordinating with utility adds weeks
   VERDICT: Plan for 4-6 week project minimum

❌ MYTH 8: "House has power during service upgrade"
✅ REALITY: House loses ALL power during work (1-3 days)
   REASON: Meter must be disconnected before work starts
   IMPACT: No refrigeration, AC, water pump during outage
   PLANNING: Customer must arrange accordingly
   VERDICT: Significant lifestyle disruption during upgrade
