LEVEL 2 CHARGING (240V Dedicated Circuit) - STANDARD HOME CHARGING
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DESCRIPTION:
• Hardwired or outlet-connected 240V charging system
• Most common residential EV charger (~90% of home installations)
• Practical overnight charging (full charge 8-12 hours)

SPECIFICATIONS (Source: 2022 CEC 625.52 [web:82]):

Voltage: 240V single-phase (same as electric dryer/range)
Amperage: Typically 40A-50A circuit (30A minimum, up to 80A possible)
Wire gauge: Depends on distance from panel
  <50 feet: #8 AWG typically
  50-100 feet: #6 AWG typically
  >100 feet: #4 or larger AWG
Breaker: Dedicated (not shared with other loads)
GFCI protection: Required (30mA maximum trip) - per CEC 625.16
Manual disconnect: Required accessible means per CEC 625.19
Connector: SAE J1772 (standard plug)
Equipment: AC Level 2 EVSE (charging equipment)

CHARGING SPEED:
Range added: ~10-30 miles per hour (depends on amperage)
Full charge time: 6-12 hours typical (250-mile vehicle)
Practical use: Overnight charge from depleted to full

PERMIT REQUIREMENT:
YES - PERMIT REQUIRED
Reason: New circuit, new breaker, new wire = electrical work
Process: EPIC-LA online application
Fee: $150-300 estimated (2023)
  Base fee: $36.00
  EV charger specific: ~$100-250 (verify current)
Contact for exact rate: (626) 458-3180

CIRCUIT REQUIREMENTS:

Breaker Sizing:
• Typical residential: 40A or 50A breaker
• Based on charger specifications
• Must match charger nameplate rating
• Oversizing not allowed (safety hazard)

Wire Sizing (important for safety):
• Distance from panel to charger location
• <50 feet: #8 AWG (typical garage)
• 50-100 feet: #6 AWG (driveway 75 feet away)
• >100 feet: #4 or larger (consider service upgrade)

GFCI Protection:
• GFCI breaker at main panel (recommended for whole circuit)
• OR GFCI protection at charger outlet
• 30mA maximum trip threshold (standard)
• Protects against ground faults (water, moisture)

Manual Disconnect:
• Allows isolation for maintenance/emergency
• Can be dedicated switch or accessible breaker
• Must be within sight of charger location
• Usually the breaker at main panel serves this purpose

⚡ LEVEL 2 CONTRACTOR QUICK TIP:
• This is the MAIN MARKET - 90% of residential installations
• Plan project carefully:
  1. Measure distance from panel to charger location
  2. Determine wire gauge needed (affects cost)
  3. Get permit quote from LA County: (626) 458-3180
  4. Include permit cost in customer bid
  5. Schedule inspection early (1-4 week wait - see File 4)
• Upsell: Offer surge protection ($50-100 add-on)
• Future: Offer app-enabled chargers ($1,500+ units) for tech-savvy customers

🚨 INSPECTOR FOCUS - LEVEL 2 CHECKLIST:
• Verifies correct breaker amperage (matches charger & wire)
• Tests wire gauge (uses spec sheet verification)
• Tests GFCI functionality (trip test with test button)
• Checks manual disconnect accessibility
• Verifies proper grounding (continuity test)
• Tests SAE J1772 connector (proper seating, no damage)
• Checks labeling ("Warning High Voltage" visible)
• Verifies no improvised installation

💼 FIELD IMPACT - LEVEL 2:
• Most profitable EV charger work
• Repeatable installation (similar for each home)
• Good markup opportunity (specialty work)
• Growing market (2+ million EVs = growth potential)
• Typical project: $1,350-4,500 (good revenue)
• Inspection usually passes with proper pre-planning

COMMON LEVEL 2 MISTAKES (Prevention):

❌ MISTAKE 1: Using undersized wire (cost cutting)
   → Safety hazard (overheating, fire risk)
   → Inspector will fail (must use correct gauge)
   → Fix: Replace wire with correct gauge
   PREVENTION: Use spec sheet, never guess wire size

❌ MISTAKE 2: Sharing circuit with other loads
   → Not allowed (breaker is for charger only)
   → Inspector will fail (violation)
   → Fix: Install new dedicated circuit
   PREVENTION: Install new breaker, don't tap existing

❌ MISTAKE 3: Wrong breaker amperage
   → Undersized: Won't work properly
   → Oversized: Overheating/fire hazard
   → Inspector will fail (verify against charger nameplate)
   PREVENTION: Match breaker to charger specifications exactly

❌ MISTAKE 4: Missing GFCI protection
   → Not optional (code requirement)
   → Inspector will fail (must add)
   → Fix: Install GFCI breaker
   PREVENTION: Specify GFCI breaker in design

❌ MISTAKE 5: Improper grounding
   → No ground connection to service ground
   → Inspector will test (must verify continuity)
   → Fix: Connect ground properly
   PREVENTION: Use proper grounding practices (same as dryer circuit)

STEP-BY-STEP LEVEL 2 PERMIT PROCESS (Ready to follow):

STEP 1: ASSESS PROJECT (Before applying)
□ Measure distance from panel to charger location
□ Determine charger specifications (brand/model)
□ Identify wire gauge needed (distance-based)
□ Calculate total project cost (see cost breakdown section)

□ Select: "New Permit" or "Electrical Work"
□ Category: "Electric Vehicle Charging Station" (or similar)
□ Provide details:
  □ Property address
  □ Charger specifications (model, amperage)
  □ Breaker size (40A or 50A)
  □ Wire gauge (#8, #6, etc.)
  □ Distance from panel
  □ Location (garage, driveway, carport)
  □ Owner contact info

STEP 3: DOCUMENT PREPARATION (Optional but recommended)
□ Take photos of:
  □ Service panel location
  □ Proposed charger location
  □ Distance to measure (for reference)
□ Get charger specs from manufacturer (nameplate or manual)
□ Calculate wire requirements (distance × gauge)

STEP 4: PAYMENT & SUBMISSION
□ Enter payment method (credit card typically)
□ Submit application
□ Receive confirmation email (keep for records)
□ Note: Permit number and reference

STEP 5: APPROVAL TIMELINE
□ LA County reviews application: 2-5 days typical
□ Simple projects: Approved within 2-3 days
□ Complex projects: May take up to 10 days
□ If questions: LA County will contact you
□ Approval email received (print for installation team)

STEP 6: INSTALLATION (After permit approved)
□ Electrician installs circuit:
  □ New breaker installed at panel
  □ Wire run from panel to charger location
  □ Charger outlet or hardwire installed
  □ Manual disconnect installed
  □ Grounding verified
□ Pre-inspection testing:
  □ GFCI test function verified
  □ Breaker operation tested
  □ Grounding continuity checked
□ Request final inspection via EPIC-LA

STEP 7: FINAL INSPECTION (1-4 weeks wait - see File 4 for seasonal timing)
□ Inspector arrives at property
□ Inspector verifies:
  □ Correct breaker amperage
  □ Wire gauge correct
  □ GFCI functionality (test button works)
  □ Manual disconnect accessible
  □ Proper grounding
  □ SAE J1772 connector (or approved type)
  □ Labeling present ("Warning High Voltage")
  □ No code violations
□ If PASS: Inspection sign-off received
□ If FAIL: Corrections required, re-inspection scheduled

STEP 8: COMPLETION
□ Homeowner can now use EV charger
□ Inspection sign-off document filed with county
□ Keep records for future reference/selling property

EV CHARGER - PRE-INSPECTION CHECKLIST (Ready to Use)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🛠️ COMPLETE PRE-INSPECTION PROCEDURE - Day Before Inspection:

CHECKLIST SECTION 1: BREAKER VERIFICATION
□ Locate breaker at main panel (should be new 40A or 50A)
□ Verify breaker amperage matches charger spec (not over/under-sized)
□ Verify breaker is dedicated (not shared with other loads)
□ Test breaker ON/OFF function (operates smoothly)
□ Verify breaker is labeled "EV CHARGER" or similar
□ Confirm breaker location is accessible (for manual disconnect)
□ Document: "Breaker size verified and operational"

CHECKLIST SECTION 2: WIRE & CIRCUIT VERIFICATION
□ Locate wire run from panel to charger location
□ Verify wire gauge matches design spec (#8, #6, etc.)
□ Wire should be inside conduit (protection from damage)
□ Check for any damage to wire or insulation
□ Verify wire routing is safe (not kinked, pinched, or exposed)
□ Check connections at both ends (panel and charger) are tight
□ Document: "Wire gauge and routing verified correct"

CHECKLIST SECTION 3: GFCI VERIFICATION
□ Locate GFCI protection (GFCI breaker at panel recommended)
□ Test GFCI functionality:
  □ Plug lamp or tester into charger outlet
  □ Lamp should be ON (charger circuit powered)
  □ Press TEST button on GFCI breaker
  □ Lamp should turn OFF immediately
  □ Press RESET button on GFCI breaker
  □ Lamp should turn back ON
  □ Repeat test 2 more times (all should work)
□ Document: "GFCI tested and operational"

CHECKLIST SECTION 4: CHARGER INSTALLATION VERIFICATION
□ Charger unit is properly mounted
□ Charger outlet box is securely installed
□ No damage to charger unit
□ All connections are tight (shake connection lightly)
□ SAE J1772 connector (or approved type) is properly seated
□ Connector has no corrosion or damage
□ Weatherproofing (if outdoor) is intact
□ Document: "Charger installed properly and secure"

CHECKLIST SECTION 5: GROUNDING & BONDING VERIFICATION
□ Verify green or bare copper ground wire present
□ Ground wire should go to service ground (at panel)
□ Continuity test ground wire (if meter available):
  □ Meter set to continuity/resistance
  □ Connect to charger ground and panel ground
  □ Should read <1 ohm (good connection)
□ Document: "Grounding verified to service ground"

CHECKLIST SECTION 6: LABELING VERIFICATION
□ "Warning High Voltage" label visible on charger
□ Breaker labeled "EV CHARGER" at panel
□ Manual disconnect clearly marked (if separate switch)
□ All labels are legible and secure
□ Document: "All required labeling in place"

CHECKLIST SECTION 7: MANUAL DISCONNECT VERIFICATION
□ Manual disconnect accessible (usually the breaker itself)
□ Disconnect operates smoothly (ON/OFF)
□ Within sight of charger location (not hidden)
□ Allows safe isolation for maintenance
□ Document: "Manual disconnect verified accessible"

CHECKLIST SECTION 8: FINAL VERIFICATION
□ All work appears code-compliant (no improvised connections)
□ No damage to wiring, charger, or connections
□ No exposed live parts
□ No water infiltration (if outdoor)
□ Everything professional and complete
□ Document: "Installation complete and code-compliant"

EV CHARGER - COMMON FAILURES & PREVENTION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

❌ FAILURE 1: "Breaker amperage doesn't match charger"
CAUSE: Wrong size breaker installed (oversized for cost savings)
CONSEQUENCE: Fire hazard or charger won't work
PREVENTION: Always verify breaker matches charger nameplate exactly
FIX: Replace breaker with correct amperage
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-4 weeks (depending on season - see File 4)

❌ FAILURE 2: "Wire gauge too small (cost cutting)"
CAUSE: Using smaller gauge wire to save money
CONSEQUENCE: Voltage drop + charger underperformance + fire risk
PREVENTION: Use distance calculations to determine correct gauge
FIX: Replace wire with correct gauge (major expense)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-4 weeks + major rework cost

❌ FAILURE 3: "GFCI not functioning"
CAUSE: Faulty GFCI breaker or improper installation
CONSEQUENCE: No ground fault protection = hazard
PREVENTION: Test GFCI day before inspection
FIX: Replace GFCI breaker ($40-60)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-4 weeks

❌ FAILURE 4: "Shared circuit with other loads"
CAUSE: Tapping existing breaker instead of new dedicated circuit
CONSEQUENCE: Insufficient power for charger + other loads
PREVENTION: Install NEW dedicated breaker, don't share
FIX: Install new circuit (if space at panel allows)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-4 weeks + possible service upgrade cost

❌ FAILURE 5: "Wrong connector type"
CAUSE: Using non-standard SAE J1772 connector
CONSEQUENCE: Incompatible with most vehicles
PREVENTION: Verify connector is SAE J1772 (standard)
FIX: Replace connector ($200-400)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-4 weeks

❌ FAILURE 6: "Missing or improper grounding"
CAUSE: Not connected to service ground
CONSEQUENCE: No fault protection = electrical hazard
PREVENTION: Verify ground wire to service ground
FIX: Install proper ground connection
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: 1-4 weeks

❌ FAILURE 7: "Missing "Warning High Voltage" label"
CAUSE: Overlooked labeling requirement
CONSEQUENCE: Safety hazard marker missing
PREVENTION: Add label before inspection
FIX: Add label ($5-10, simple)
RE-INSPECTION WAIT: Usually no re-inspection needed (minor fix)

PREVENTION STRATEGY:
→ Use pre-inspection checklist (above) DAY BEFORE inspection
→ Test all systems with meter/equipment
→ Fix any problems found BEFORE inspector arrives
→ Result: PASS first inspection, no re-inspection delays

EV CHARGER - MYTHS vs REALITY
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

❌ MYTH 1: "I can use my dryer outlet for EV charger"
✅ REALITY: NO - Cannot share outlets/circuits
   REASON: Charger requires DEDICATED circuit (not shared)
   CONSEQUENCE: Insufficient power, unsafe, inspector fails
   SOLUTION: Install new dedicated 240V circuit for charger
   VERDICT: Must install new circuit - no sharing allowed

❌ MYTH 2: "Level 1 (120V) is fine for daily use"
✅ REALITY: Level 1 too slow for practical daily driving
   REASON: Only 2-3 miles range per hour = 48 hours for full charge
   VERDICT: Level 1 only for emergency/backup charging
   SOLUTION: Install Level 2 for practical daily use

❌ MYTH 3: "I can install this myself (DIY)"
✅ REALITY: Recommend licensed electrician (safety/code)
   REASON: High voltage work, code compliance, inspection requirement
   RISK: Fire hazard if improper installation
   CONSEQUENCE: Inspector may reject DIY work
   VERDICT: Hire licensed electrician for safety

❌ MYTH 4: "GFCI not needed for EV charger"
✅ REALITY: GFCI is CODE REQUIRED (not optional)
   REASON: Ground fault protection is safety critical
   CONSEQUENCE: Inspector will fail without GFCI
   VERDICT: GFCI is mandatory - no exceptions

❌ MYTH 5: "Any 240V circuit works (like dryer)"
✅ REALITY: Charger circuit must be DEDICATED and correct gauge
   REASON: Charger amperage must match wire + breaker specs
   CONSEQUENCE: Wrong gauge = voltage drop + fire hazard
   PREVENTION: Use proper calculations for distance/amperage
   VERDICT: Circuit must be specifically sized for charger

❌ MYTH 6: "Old home chargers work in new vehicles"
✅ REALITY: Connector standards changing (Tesla NACS emerging)
   REASON: Tesla introducing new NACS standard (replacing SAE J1772)
   FACT: Current standard = SAE J1772 (almost all non-Tesla)
   FACT: Tesla converting to NACS (2024+)
   FUTURE: May need adapter or new charger
   VERDICT: Verify charger compatibility with vehicle

❌ MYTH 7: "EV charger installation is simple electrical work"
✅ REALITY: Specialized work requiring EV charging knowledge
   REASON: Specific code requirements (Article 625), connector types, disconnects
   REQUIREMENT: Electrician should have EV charger experience
   VERDICT: Not simple - treat as specialized work
